# Bridge World Standard 2017: the `bws` preset

**Status: built 2026-11-26, measured 2026-11-27, attributed and named
2026-11-28 ([§5](#5-measurement)): the preset loses to `advanced` in every
cell, as expected; its one new knob (`rebid.semiforcing_notrump`) won 4/4
alone and ships default-on.** `Agreements::bws()`, `--ns-preset bws` in
`bba-gen`, `render-book` and `probe-call-reading`, the pin
`bws_differs_from_default_only_on_the_listed_fields` and
`scripts/ab-bws.sh` are on `main`. With the verdict read, the name's surface
followed `basic`'s: the factory `american_bws()`, the golden
`cards/American-BWS.bbsa` (`bba-card --system american-bws`, pinned by
`the_checked_in_cards_match_the_generator`) and the `bws` variant of the web
`system_preset` row. The card differs from `cards/American.bbsa` in three
rows (Multi-Landy on, Rubensohl after double off, Super acceptance after NT
on); EPBot's schema has no row for the other flips. Until the semiforcing
flip the default system was byte-identical (`smoke-default --count 20000
--seed 1` against a clean `75b2135a` build), and the naming touches no
default path.

The spec is public: <https://bridgeworld.com/bridge-world-standard> (fetched
2026-11-26; section numbers below are its own). It is "common American expert
practices, determined by polls", 2/1 with a semiforcing `1NT`.

## 1. What the preset is

A third named `Agreements` value beside `basic` and `advanced`
([system-variants.md §2](system-variants.md#2-mechanism--a-variant-is-an-agreements-value-not-a-factory)):
`default()` with every knob moved to the state BWS prescribes, **where that
state is authored**. Three rules kept it small:

- **A knob moves only to an authored state that says what BWS says.** Where
  BWS's meaning is unauthored the shipped state stays and the row is a hole
  ([§4](#4-holes-ranked)), even when the shipped state contradicts BWS
  (`1M - 3NT`, the reversed unusual-vs-unusual cues over a minor). An off-state
  that falls to the floor is not BWS either.
- **Numeric ranges are untouched** (Tier B of
  [bidding-options.md](bidding-options.md)), as in `basic`. BWS states few and
  ours are measured.
- **The default is the measured card, BWS the polled one.** Most flips undo a
  measured verdict, so the expected result is a loss; the preset prices
  expert consensus, it is not a claim to beat `advanced`.

`advanced` is already most of BWS. The census ([§3](#3-conformance-census))
classed about 60 agreements `SAME` before any flip: 2/1 game forcing, limit
raises, Jacoby `2NT` with shortness rebids, splinters, inverted minors, the
Puppet `2♠` range-or-clubs and `2NT` diamonds over `1NT`, garbage Stayman,
Smolen, transfer autosplinters, XYZ after the `1NT` rebid, Jordan `2NT`,
support doubles, the Modern negative double, forcing free bids, cue raises,
Michaels and the unusual `2NT`, Leaping Michaels, systems on over our `1NT`
overcall, Reverse Drury.

Fidelity notes on flips that are close, not exact:

- **Plain lebensohl is not "fast denies".** `Plain`'s direct cue is
  stopper-agnostic and its direct `3NT` *requires* a stopper; BWS has both
  deny one, showing it through `2NT`. `competition.delayed_cue` is the
  slow-shows half but is wired for `Transfer` only, and `2NT - 3♣ - 3NT` is
  unauthored under either style.
- **The strong jump shift** is BBA's shape (5+ spades, at most two hearts),
  not BWS's three hand types, and has no two-honour test.
- **Woolsey** has no `2♦` major ask over the double (the ask is a game-going
  `2NT`), passes a six-card major with a five-card minor, and authors no
  natural three-level overcall.
- **The transfer defense's** indicated-suit bid is Michaels (the other major
  and a minor), not a plain takeout; the Stayman double wants five clubs with
  honours where BWS says "strength unspecified".
- **`strong_two_loose_positive` off** wants two of the top three honours in a
  five-card suit; BWS also admits any six-card suit.

Considered and left alone, because no state is BWS: `decision.reading.two_notrump_wide`
(drops the five-card major BWS allows), `opening.longer_suit_first` (BWS opens
the higher suit only when the two are adjacent), `opening.weak_two_wild`,
`rebid.two_over_one_reverse_extras` (BWS wants extras after `1♥ - 2m - 2♠`
and none after `1♦ - 2♣ - 2M`; one bool), `notrump.two_notrump_transfer_rebid`
(its `4M` is to play, BWS's a slam try), `competition.uvu_over_minors` (cues
reversed), `defense.notrump_balancing_enabled` (balancing-seat advances
unauthored), `defense.weak_two_cue` (Michaels, BWS's is a stopper ask),
the `2♠`/`2NT` transfer defenses (BWS names only the general rule).

## 4. Holes, ranked

Each is an `author-convention` ticket on `american()`, measured alone; one
that wins joins `bws()` and, if it wins outright, the default. By frequency
at the table:

1. **Advances of our simple overcall** (VI.C). The most common defensive
   continuation is the floor's; `rubens_advances` is a different scheme and a
   measured loss.
2. **Sandwich and reopening seats** (VI.A, D, E, G). No node; the reopening
   `1NT` 10–14 and the systems-on graft under it are one table.
3. **Round two after our response is overcalled or doubled** (V.F).
4. **XYZ confined to the `1NT` rebid** (IV.I), with natural invitations and a
   fourth-suit game force after a suit rebid. The largest `DIFFERS` in IV.I;
   the off-state of `xyz` authors nothing, so this is new tables, not a flip.
5. **Texas at `4♦`/`4♥` with Gerber** (IV.E, IV.F). A relocation with every
   contested copy; Gerber was rejected once.
6. **0314 replies** (III). Touches every asker table, 27 floor sites, the
   readers and the nets' training distribution: retrain-gated, and
   double-dummy sees only the queen-ask step.
7. **The takeout double's 10-count** (VI.A): one floor knob over `hcp(12..)`.
8. **Jump shifts** (IV.G(f), IV.H): invitational `1M - 3x`, the five-four
   majors `1m - 2♥`/`2♠`, strong `1♣ - 2♦`.
9. **`1NT - 3♣`/`3♦` for the minors** (IV.F(e)): needs a third
   `notrump_minors` scheme and a new home for Puppet Stayman.
10. **Kokish, the second negative, the feature ask, fast-denies lebensohl,
    the stopper-ask cue of a weak two**: each one table and its answers.

generated from its own agreements, four cells:

- **`bws` vs `advanced`: a loss in every cell on both scorers**, 11–12% of
  boards diverging, −0.10 to −0.21 plain per fired. The price of the
  consensus card is about 0.02 IMPs a board plain and 0.10 PD; the PD
  column is five times the plain one, so most of it is in the doubling
  bracket. The worst plain boards are mostly **BBA's own call differing over
  the other card** (a disclosure effect, as `basic`'s were): in
  `2♦ - 3♦ (3♥) - (4♦) X (XX)` BBA sits for the redouble where against
  `advanced` it runs to `4♥`, and over `1♠ - 2NT (4NT)` it picks another
  five-level answer to double. Attribution below.
- **Attribution** (pueue task 44, `ab-results/bws/bucket.{side,node}.*.txt`,
  read 2026-11-28). By side, auctions we open carry 49 / 63 / 45 / 70% of
  the plain loss (none / ns / ew / both) and 60–68% of PD; auctions they
  open carry the rest. BBA's own divergences net to about zero. By node the
  loss is diffuse: 39–47 thousand first-divergence nodes a cell, the top 40
  carry 14–17% of PD. Among those 40, two flips stand out:
  - **Woolsey** (`decision.reading.notrump_defense`) is the largest plain
    cost: −3,281 / −2,161 / −4,399 / −3,032 IMPs over their `1NT`
    (`- 1NT - vs 2♦`, `1NT 2♦ vs 2♠`, `1NT X vs 2♦`, …), i.e. 19 / 11 /
    45 / 20% of the whole plain loss from a handful of nodes. It costs more
    plain than PD per fired board (−1.1 plain on the commonest node), so it
    is bidding, not the doubling bracket. This repeats the recorded loss.
  - **Our weak two in competition** (`competition.weak_two_competition`:
    responder's `X` over their overcall, `3M`/`4M` vs pass over their
    double) is the largest PD cost: −4,258 / −5,513 / −5,075 / −6,525 PD
    against −1,044 / −2,313 / −1,538 / −2,937 plain, at −4 to −9 PD per
    fired board. That is the recorded "plain wash, PD loss", sharper here.
  - Smaller: the responsive double (`1x 1y 2x X`) at −0.04 to −0.7k plain,
    the strict `2♣` positive (`- 2♣ - 2♦ vs 3♦`) at −0.2 to −0.5k, the
    strong `1♥ - 2♠` (responder passing a weak jump shift) at −0.2k.
  The rest of the we-open half (Stayman answers, super-accept, plain
  lebensohl, takeout doubles of overcalls of `1NT`, high-overcall
  responses) sits in the long tail, below any single top-40 node. Each of
