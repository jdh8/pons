# Counter-defenses to a Muiderberg-style `1NT (2M)` overcall

**Research date:** 2026-08-25. **Auctions:** our side opens a strong `1NT`;
the next opponent overcalls `2♥` or `2♠`; the bid promises five or more cards
in that major and an as-yet-unknown minor. In pons's BBA lane the promise is narrower:
exactly five in the major and four or more in a minor.

## Scope and source discipline

This is the counter-defense used by the **1NT opener's side** after
`1NT (2♥)` or `1NT (2♠)`. Three nearby objects are deliberately excluded:

- A Muiderberg **opening** `(2♥)` or `(2♠)` is a different auction: there is no
  strong-notrump opener and different calls are available. Opening-defense
  cards are not evidence for this report.
- `1NT (2♥)` showing **both majors** is a two-known-suit method, commonly a
  DONT treatment. It changes both double and stopper logic. It is not silently
  transposed here.
- `1NT (2M)` in which the bid is a transfer and does **not** promise the bid
  major is also a different object. The central fact here is that the major
  actually bid is known.

The source order is:

1. named partnership/system notes and filed championship cards;
2. authored expert methods;
3. authored teaching material;
4. expert-panel testimony as evidence about disputed choices, not as a source
   from which to splice a complete method.

External auctions have been normalized to pons notation: every pass is `-`,
and only opponents' non-pass calls are parenthesized. None of the reviewed
sources supplies controlled IMP evidence comparing the methods. The literature
supports authoring coherent candidates; it does not supply a ship verdict.

## Result in brief

The strongest common finding is a **classification rule**, not a particular
call table: when the overcall shows the bid major plus one unknown minor, treat
the known major as the anchor suit for first-round natural calls and stopper
logic. Do **not** treat the call as a bid showing both known suits, and do not
ask for a stopper in an unidentified minor.

The credible methods then divide into several families. These are not merely
different names for the same table: they spend `X`, `2NT`, the cue and the top
three-level step differently.

| Family | Immediate `X` | Main machinery | Best evidence | Principal open seam |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kokish–Kraft bespoke transfers | negative, competitive/invitational+ | minor transfers, special two-suit shapes, both minors, explicit game-level calls | exact tables on printed pp. 165 and 167 | a complete opener table after `X`, and one ambiguous minor continuation |
| Pahk–Zelevinsky transfer Lebensohl | takeout, strong enough to be converted | weak/slow `2NT`, transfers, cue-Stayman, both minors | exact semantic rule on pp. 4–5 | action after the opponents reveal the minor |
| Standard Lebensohl | partnership choice: penalty-oriented or negative | `2NT` relay, fast/slow strength and stopper splits | Dubois pp. 103–105; Walker | the double style and all hidden-minor tails |
| Kantar transfers | Stayman with a stopper in the known major | transfers from `2NT`, with slow stopperless Stayman/`3NT` and a penalty trap pass | Kantar's exact-object authored table | reopening-double threshold and interfered tails |
| Bridge World Standard | penalty suggestion against the eventual escape | ordinary Lebensohl using the known major as anchor | expert-poll consensus standard | it is a process, not a complete seat-by-seat escape table |
| Current filed methods | negative or values/cards | transfer Lebensohl or plain Lebensohl | Fleisher–Martel 2024; Purdy–James 2026 | several continuations are undisclosed |
| Rubensohl | values | transfers beginning at `2NT`; transfer into their suit handles Stayman/stopperless `3NT` | Omran–Yousry filed notes, pp. 1–2 | opener's transfer completions and competitive tails |
| BBA empirical method | four or more cards in the other major | plain Lebensohl | local EPBot probe | prose specification and interfered continuations |

There is no consensus on double. Published meanings include penalty,
cooperative penalty, values/cards, negative, broad takeout, and specifically
the other major; Kantar instead uses `X` as Stayman with a stopper in the known
major. That is a real design choice, not terminological drift.

The two overcalls are not mechanically symmetric. After `(2♥)`, responder
still has a natural non-forcing `2♠`; after `(2♠)`, hearts have already been
pushed to the three level. Kokish–Kraft and Kantar consequently publish
different tables for the two anchors. A pons package should share principles,
not generate one lane from the other by blind suit substitution.

## Exact-object methods

### 1. Kokish–Kraft: a separate table for each known major

The Eric Kokish–Beverly Kraft system notes expressly group a natural major and
a major-plus-unknown-minor overcall together. Their `2♥` heading includes both
`4+♥/5+m` and `5+♥/4+m`; their `2♠` heading includes `4+♠/4+m`. This is the
target semantic object, not a defense imported from a Muiderberg opening
([Kokish–Kraft, printed pp. 165 and 167](https://bridgewithdan.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/WEAK-NOTRUMP-SYSTEM-Kokish-Kraft-Jan-2008.pdf)).

The source is a weak-notrump system and the copy found is an unaffiliated
mirror. Its strength labels therefore do not transfer automatically to pons's
15–17 opening.

| Our call | After `1NT (2♥)` | After `1NT (2♠)` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `-` | Neutral; a delayed `X` is penalty, delayed `2NT` is competitive and both-minor, and delayed `3m` is competitive ([p. 165](https://bridgewithdan.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/WEAK-NOTRUMP-SYSTEM-Kokish-Kraft-Jan-2008.pdf)). | Neutral; a delayed `X` is penalty and delayed `3♣`/`4♣` is competitive ([p. 167](https://bridgewithdan.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/WEAK-NOTRUMP-SYSTEM-Kokish-Kraft-Jan-2008.pdf)). |
| `X` | Negative, competitive or better. A later double of hearts is cooperative takeout; later doubles of other suits are penalty; `2NT` later is invitational ([p. 165](https://bridgewithdan.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/WEAK-NOTRUMP-SYSTEM-Kokish-Kraft-Jan-2008.pdf)). | Negative, invitational or better. Later doubles are cooperative takeout; `3♠` asks for a stopper ([p. 167](https://bridgewithdan.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/WEAK-NOTRUMP-SYSTEM-Kokish-Kraft-Jan-2008.pdf)). |
| `2♠` | Natural and competitive ([p. 165](https://bridgewithdan.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/WEAK-NOTRUMP-SYSTEM-Kokish-Kraft-Jan-2008.pdf)). | Not available. |
| `2NT` | Transfer to clubs, invitational or better. Follow-ups distinguish clubs plus spades, clubs plus diamonds, and one-suited clubs with partial/full heart stoppers ([p. 165](https://bridgewithdan.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/WEAK-NOTRUMP-SYSTEM-Kokish-Kraft-Jan-2008.pdf)). | Multi-purpose: competitive-or-better clubs; a competitive diamond or heart hand via opener's `3♣`; or specified game-forcing club/heart and club-plus-stopper hands ([p. 167](https://bridgewithdan.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/WEAK-NOTRUMP-SYSTEM-Kokish-Kraft-Jan-2008.pdf)). |
| `3♣` | Diamonds, invitational or better ([p. 165](https://bridgewithdan.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/WEAK-NOTRUMP-SYSTEM-Kokish-Kraft-Jan-2008.pdf)). | Transfer to diamonds, invitational or better ([p. 167](https://bridgewithdan.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/WEAK-NOTRUMP-SYSTEM-Kokish-Kraft-Jan-2008.pdf)). |
| `3♦` | The source writes `4=0=4=5`: four spades, no hearts, four diamonds and five clubs ([p. 165](https://bridgewithdan.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/WEAK-NOTRUMP-SYSTEM-Kokish-Kraft-Jan-2008.pdf)). | Transfer to hearts, invitational or better ([p. 167](https://bridgewithdan.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/WEAK-NOTRUMP-SYSTEM-Kokish-Kraft-Jan-2008.pdf)). |
| `3♥` | Shows spades, invitational or better ([p. 165](https://bridgewithdan.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/WEAK-NOTRUMP-SYSTEM-Kokish-Kraft-Jan-2008.pdf)). | The source writes `0=4=(54)`: no spades, four hearts, and five-four in the minors, game forcing ([p. 167](https://bridgewithdan.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/WEAK-NOTRUMP-SYSTEM-Kokish-Kraft-Jan-2008.pdf)). |
| `3♠` | Both minors, game forcing ([p. 165](https://bridgewithdan.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/WEAK-NOTRUMP-SYSTEM-Kokish-Kraft-Jan-2008.pdf)). | Both minors, game forcing ([p. 167](https://bridgewithdan.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/WEAK-NOTRUMP-SYSTEM-Kokish-Kraft-Jan-2008.pdf)). |
| `3NT` | Natural, no heart stopper, probably based on a long minor; a balanced high-card hand begins with `X` ([p. 165](https://bridgewithdan.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/WEAK-NOTRUMP-SYSTEM-Kokish-Kraft-Jan-2008.pdf)). | Natural; if based on a minor, probably no spade stopper ([p. 167](https://bridgewithdan.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/WEAK-NOTRUMP-SYSTEM-Kokish-Kraft-Jan-2008.pdf)). |
| `4♣`; `4♦`/`4♥`; `4♠` | Gerber; Texas; both minors weak and very long, respectively ([p. 165](https://bridgewithdan.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/WEAK-NOTRUMP-SYSTEM-Kokish-Kraft-Jan-2008.pdf)). | The same allocations ([p. 167](https://bridgewithdan.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/WEAK-NOTRUMP-SYSTEM-Kokish-Kraft-Jan-2008.pdf)). |

The `2♠` table goes further than most sources: after `2NT - 3♣`, opener can
make a normal `3♣` completion or an accepting `3♦`, and responder then sorts
competitive diamonds/hearts from stronger club hands. It still is not a full
tree after opponents compete. The `2♥` table also appears to duplicate one
step in the `3♣` continuation description; that line should be checked against
the partnership before being encoded. This is a source-text uncertainty, not a
call meaning to repair by inference.

### 2. Pahk–Zelevinsky: use the known major as a natural anchor

Joon Pahk and Leo Zelevinsky's *Transfer Oriented Symmetric Relay Precision
Competitive* notes give the cleanest general rule. If an overcall shows the
bid suit plus another **unknown** suit, treat the bid as natural; use systems-on
over `2♣`, and Lebensohl or transfer Lebensohl over `2♦`/`2M`. If both suits are
known but neither is known to be longer, they instead begin with a
penalty-oriented double. The notes use DONT `2♥` as that contrasting two-known-
suit example
([Pahk–Zelevinsky, printed p. 4](https://www.bantha.org/~pahk/bridge/tosr/TOSR_Competitive.pdf)).

Their exact `1NT (2M)` transfer-Lebensohl table is
([Pahk–Zelevinsky, printed p. 5](https://www.bantha.org/~pahk/bridge/tosr/TOSR_Competitive.pdf)):

| `1NT (2M) ?` | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `-` | May be a trap pass; opener should reopen with shortness in the bid major. |
| `X` | Takeout, with enough strength to be converted by opener. Usually the other major below game force, but may be a minor two-suiter planning to convert opener's response. |
| `2♠` | To play, when available over `(2♥)`. |
| `2NT` | A competitive one-suiter below their major, or the slow stopper route to the cue/`3NT`; opener bids `3♣`, pass or correct. |
| `3♣` | Diamonds, invitational or better. Opener's `3♦` declines; another descriptive action accepts and forces game. |
| `3♦` | Five or more in the other major, invitational or better. Completing three of that major declines; anything else is descriptive and game forcing. |
| `3M` | Stayman without a stopper in their known major. |
| `3OM` | Both minors, at least 5–5, game forcing. |
| `3NT` | Denies a stopper in their known major. |
| `4♣` | Clubs plus the other major, at least 5–5, game forcing. |
| `4♦`/`4♥` | Texas remains on; the transfer **into their major** instead shows diamonds plus the other major, at least 5–5. |
| `4♠` and above | The partnership's uncontested meanings. |

This is a coherent complete first round. It also states opener's trap-pass
reopening duty and how to convert the takeout double. It does **not** state
what either partner does after the overcalling side uses `2NT` to ask for its
minor or names that minor under pressure.

### 3. Dubois: ordinary Lebensohl, with the unknown minor ignored initially

Paul F. Dubois's authored *Bridge Bidding* says exactly that when an overcall
shows one definite suit plus an unknown second suit, the second suit is ignored
for the moment. Its example is Cappelletti `2♥` showing hearts plus a minor;
the partnership reacts as though hearts alone had been bid
([Dubois, printed p. 104](https://pfdubois.github.io/BridgeBidding/BridgeBidding.pdf)).

The surrounding Lebensohl method supplies the full direct skeleton
([Dubois, printed pp. 103–105](https://pfdubois.github.io/BridgeBidding/BridgeBidding.pdf)):

| Call | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Two-level new suit | Natural, non-forcing, to play. |
| Direct three-level new suit | Natural, game forcing. |
| `2NT` | Relay to `3♣`; pass with weak clubs, sign off below their suit, or show an invitation in a higher-ranking suit. |
| Direct `3M` | Stayman without a stopper in the known major. |
| `2NT - 3♣ - 3M` | Stayman with a stopper in the known major. |
| Direct `3NT` | Game values, no unbid four-card major, no stopper in the known major. |
| `2NT - 3♣ - 3NT` | Game values, no unbid four-card major, with a stopper in the known major. |
| `4♣`; `4♦`/`4♥`; `4♠`; `4NT` | Gerber; Texas; ask opener to choose a minor game; quantitative with a stopper. |

The book presents two competing double styles rather than pretending there is
one standard: penalty-oriented is the traditional treatment, while a newer
approach uses a negative double. Its simplified version chooses a
penalty-oriented double with at least two cards in their suit
([Dubois, pp. 104–107](https://pfdubois.github.io/BridgeBidding/BridgeBidding.pdf)).

Karen Walker independently gives the same exact classification: against one
known and one unknown suit, stopper auctions revolve around the known suit;
her example again treats Cappelletti `2♥` as a regular heart overcall
([Walker, “Lebensohl after a conventional overcall”](https://kwbridge.com/leb-1nt.htm)).
Her worked `1NT (2♥)` table uses penalty `X`, weak `2♠`, the `2NT` relay,
fast-denies/slow-shows cues and `3NT`, and Texas, while explicitly noting that
some partnerships prefer a takeout double. Walker is corroborating authored
teaching evidence, not a separate outcome comparison.

### 4. Kantar: transfers, a stopper-showing `X`, and a penalty trap pass

Eddie Kantar publishes a table headed for a natural `2♥` or `2♠` overcall and
then says explicitly that it is also playable when the call shows the bid
suit plus an unknown minor. This makes it exact-object expert evidence rather
than a natural-overcall analogy
([Kantar, “After a 1NT opening bid and a natural 2H or 2S overcall”](https://www.kantarbridge.com/ideas.html)).

| Our call | After `1NT (2♥)` | After `1NT (2♠)` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `X` | Stayman with a heart stopper. | Stayman with a spade stopper. |
| natural other major | `2♠` natural, non-forcing; `3♠` natural and forcing. | `3♥` natural and forcing. |
| `2NT` | Transfer to clubs. | Transfer to clubs. |
| `3♣` | Transfer to diamonds. | Transfer to diamonds. |
| `3♦` | Transfer to spades. | Transfer to hearts. |
| cue | `3♥` shows both minors, game forcing. | `3♠` shows both minors, game forcing. |
| `3NT` | Natural with a heart stopper. | Natural with a spade stopper. |
| game level | `4♣` Gerber; `4♥` transfers to spades; `4NT` natural. | `4♣` Gerber; `4♦` transfers to hearts; `4♥` to play; `4NT` natural. |

After `1NT (2♥) 2NT - 3♣`, responder can pass with clubs. A rebid of `3♥`
cancels the club message and is Stayman without a heart stopper; `3NT`
likewise cancels clubs and shows a natural game hand without the stopper.
Kantar also supplies a penalty process: responder passes the overcall with a
penalty-oriented hand, and opener with shortness and a maximum or near maximum
reopens with `X` for conversion. The source does not enumerate the same
process after advancer asks for or names the hidden minor.

### 5. Bridge World Standard: anchor-suit Lebensohl, but double the escape

Bridge World Standard 2017 is an expert-poll consensus system, not one pair's
card. After our `1NT`, it applies ordinary Lebensohl over a two-level
overcall, with fast-denies-stopper cue and `3NT` routes. It then says that an
artificial action is treated as though it were natural in an indicated anchor
suit. A major-plus-unknown-minor `2M` therefore uses the bid major for the
Lebensohl and stopper table
([BWS 2017, §V.C](https://www.bridgeworld.com/pages/readingroom/bws/bwscompletesystem.html)).

BWS does **not** thereby turn `X` into an ordinary negative double of a
natural major. Its separate rule says that double of an artificial bid
suggests penalizing the eventual escape, after which the opening side is
forced through `2NT`; below-game new suits force, while raises and `2NT` do
not. That is a penalty process aimed at whichever strain the convention
reaches, not simply a claim that responder can punish the five-card major.
The distinction is especially relevant when advancer's `2NT` asks for the
unknown minor.

BWS also distinguishes two delayed seats explicitly: after
`1NT (2M) - -`, opener's `X` is takeout, whereas after
`1NT - - (2M)`, opener's `X` is penalty. A single generic “delayed double”
meaning would erase that source rule.

### 6. Current filed exact-object methods

Two recent championship cards explicitly include the target semantics:

- Fleisher–Martel's 2024 WBF Note 9 says their transfer Lebensohl applies when
  `2M` is one-suited **or** shows the bid major plus an unknown second suit.
  `2NT` forces `3♣`, either to stop below the major or before a `3M` rebid
  showing shortness in the major without four cards in the other major;
  direct `3♣` transfers to invitational-or-better diamonds, `3♦` to the other
  major, and `3OM` to clubs. Their main card labels doubles negative
  ([Fleisher–Martel, Note 9](https://usbf.org/docs/2026usbc/acblcards/FleisherMartelWBF2024.pdf)).
  The current note does not disclose a fast/slow `3NT` stopper split, so the
  split on their [2019 WBF card, Note 9](https://info.ecatsbridge.com/systems/2019WorldTeams-Wuhan/BermudaBowl/USA2/Fleisher-Martel.pdf)
  is not imported.
- Purdy–James's 2026 EBL card says Lebensohl applies to major-minor
  two-suiters. Their direct `X` shows about 9+ HCP with no clear call; `2NT`
  forces `3♣`; a two-level new suit is non-encouraging and a three-level new
  suit forces. Direct `3M` is game forcing with a stopper. The slow cue shows
  four cards in the other major without a stopper, while slow `3NT` shows
  game values, no stopper and fewer than four cards in that major
  ([Purdy–James, Note 3](https://championships.eurobridge.org/2026EOWT/CC/Shamrock2026_Purdy_James.pdf)).

These filed tables confirm that exact pairs independently choose different
double and stopper packages. Neither card publishes every continuation after
the opponents reveal the minor.

## Target-compatible and corroborating evidence

### 7. Omran–Yousry: filed Rubensohl with a values double

Ahmed Omran and Ahmed Yousry's supplementary notes, filed on the African
Bridge Federation site, label Note 2 “Rubensohl after 2-level overcall of
1NT.” Note 3 in the same document defines their own Multi-Landy `2♥`/`2♠` as
five in the bid major plus a four-card-or-longer minor
([card, p. 1](https://www.bridgewebs.com/abf/Omran-Yousry.pdf);
[supplement, pp. 1–2](https://www.bridgewebs.com/abf/Omran-YousryNOTES.pdf)).

The Rubensohl note is written as a general defense to a two-level overcall, not
as a section headed “against Muiderberg.” Its wording covers the target auction,
but the document does not explicitly say whether every artificial `2M` receives
the same table. That applicability caveat remains visible.

| Our call | After `1NT (2♥)` | After `1NT (2♠)` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `X` | Values. | Values. |
| `2NT` | Transfer to `3♣`. | Transfer to `3♣`. |
| `3♣` | Transfer to `3♦`. | Transfer to `3♦`. |
| next step below their suit | `3♦`: transfer into hearts, described as Stayman or a route to `3NT` without a stopper. | `3♥`: transfer into spades, described the same way. |
| other major transfer | `3♥` transfers to `3♠`. | `3♦` transfers to `3♥`. |
| `3♠` | Both minors. | Both minors. |
| `3NT` | Natural with a heart stopper. | Natural with a spade stopper. |
| game-level calls | `4♣` quantitative with five clubs or 4–4 minors; `4♥` transfers to spades; `4♠` quantitative with five diamonds; `4NT` quantitative, 15–17, any 4-3-3-3 with a stopper. | The same, except `4♦` transfers to hearts. |

These meanings are all stated in Note 2
([Omran–Yousry supplement, pp. 1–2](https://www.bridgewebs.com/abf/Omran-YousryNOTES.pdf)).
The note does not publish opener's transfer completions, strength splits after
a completion, or action over a minor-revealing bid by the opponents.

### 8. Larry Cohen: one low-memory rule, including conventional `2M`

Larry Cohen proposes one scheme for any `2♦`-or-higher overcall that does not
specifically show both majors. Two-level suits are natural and non-forcing,
three-level and notrump calls use Lebensohl or transfer Lebensohl, and
four-level calls retain the partnership's front-of-card meanings
([Cohen, “Notrump Interference – Advanced”](https://www.larryco.com/bridge-articles/notrump-interference-advanced)).

In this class, immediate `X` is cards/negative: values to act, no promise of
the other major. Pass followed by double is light takeout/competitive. A true
penalty hand passes and relies on opener to reopen with a double
([Cohen](https://www.larryco.com/bridge-articles/notrump-interference-advanced)).

That is a coherent exact-object skeleton because a major-plus-minor call does
not show both majors. It does not supply a Muiderberg-specific transfer table,
hidden-minor tail, or exact reopening threshold.

### 9. Piper–Punch: current filed evidence, but an unresolved classification

Ying Piper and Samantha Punch's filed 2026 Eurobridge card defines their own
Multi-Landy `2M` as five in the major plus four or more in a minor. Elsewhere it
says that after `1NT (2suit)`, `X` is takeout if the suit is natural and values
if the call is artificial; `2NT`/`3♣`/`3♦`/`3♥` transfer to
clubs/diamonds/hearts/spades
([Piper–Punch card, pp. 1–2](https://championships.eurobridge.org/2026EOWT/CC/CALEDONIA_PIPER_PUNCH.pdf)).

The card does not say which double branch applies to a semi-natural `2M` that
guarantees the bid major but also promises an unknown minor. It is therefore
official evidence for the values-double Rubensohl family, not proof of an exact
Muiderberg double meaning.

## Expert evidence: no consensus on `X`

Steve Robinson's NVBA panel asks experts about direct and balancing action
after a 1NT opening is disturbed, including an exact hearts-plus-minor example.
The answers establish disagreement rather than a single standard
([Robinson panel](https://www.nvba.org/Robinson/articles/defense-against-notrump.html)):

- Karen Allison uses a cooperative/penalty double at the two level, with at
  least invitational values and enough trumps for opener to decide.
- Eddie Wold says a two-suited bid that includes the suit actually bid receives
  a takeout double; a purely artificial call receives a cards double.
- Chip Martel uses a negative-type double over a one-suiter or a bid-suit-plus-
  unknown method, but cards when both suits are known.
- John Wittes and Jill Meyers give the same anchor-suit/unknown-suit distinction:
  negative over the target object, penalty over two known suits.
- Bobby Wolff describes `X` of a natural or natural-ish major as normally four
  cards in the other major with roughly invitational values, alongside
  Lebensohl.
- Other panelists prefer direct penalty or cooperative-penalty doubles as a
  general rule, and some exact hearts-plus-minor examples occur in balancing
  seat rather than directly over the call.

The direct-versus-balancing distinction matters. A panel answer to
`1NT - - (2♥ = hearts+minor) - - X` is not automatically a meaning for
`1NT (2♥) X`. The panel supports the existence of the competing philosophies;
it does not fill any missing direct continuation.

## The hidden-minor decision tree

The overcall's second suit is unknown on round one, but BBA's actual advancer
reveals it frequently. After `1NT (2M) -`, BBA's advancer chooses `(2NT)` about
half the time as an artificial minor ask; overcaller replies `(3♣)` or `(3♦)`
([BBA Multi-Landy distillation](bba-1nt-defense.md)). A complete counter-defense
therefore needs more than the first responder table.

| Position | What the sources cover | What remains missing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `1NT (2M) - - ?` | Pahk–Zelevinsky say opener reopens with shortness after a possible trap pass; Cohen's negative-double style also depends on reopening for a penalty pass. | Exact shortness/strength threshold, and opener's natural escape ordering. |
| `1NT (2M) X - ?` | Pahk–Zelevinsky make `X` convertible takeout; Kokish–Kraft give some later-double principles; BBA always acts and shows the other major when held. | No external source reviewed gives a finite opener table for every balanced shape. |
| `1NT (2M) X (2NT) ?` | None of the exact sources gives a complete table after advancer asks for the minor over our double. | Whether `X`, pass, cues and natural suits retain their first-round meaning. |
| `1NT (2M) X (3m) ?` | Kokish–Kraft distinguish some later doubles of the major from doubles of another suit. | Who owns takeout, penalty and stopper actions after the actual minor appears. |
| `1NT (2M) 2NT (3m) ?` | The first `2NT` meaning is well documented across the reviewed families. | Opener's action when the opponents bid through or above the relay/transfer. |
| `1NT (2M) - (2NT) - (3m) ?` | BBA establishes that this minor-reveal auction is common for the overcalling side. | No reviewed human source gives a complete responder reopening table here. |

This is the largest research gap. Kokish–Kraft gives the most detailed set of
delayed-double defaults; Kantar and BWS state penalty processes, but none of
them enumerates every seat after the minor ask. A first implementation must
author these tails as a partnership design and label them as such; it cannot
cite a first-round Lebensohl table as if the tails followed automatically.

## `2NT`, cues, `3NT`, both minors, and jumps

| Method | `2NT` | Known-major stopper logic | Both minors | Direct game-level calls |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

| Cohen | Lebensohl or transfer Lebensohl | no special Muiderberg table in the article | depends on chosen Lebensohl variant | normal front-of-card methods ([Cohen](https://www.larryco.com/bridge-articles/notrump-interference-advanced)) |

Two bounded conclusions follow from this source set:

1. Every exact semantic source keys stopper logic to the **known major**, not
   to both minors or to the artificiality of the whole call. No reviewed source
   asks responder to stop an unidentified minor before it is named.
2. Four rich methods reserve a high three-level rung for **both minors**:
   Kokish–Kraft use `3♠`, Pahk–Zelevinsky use `3OM`, Kantar uses the direct
   cue, and Omran–Yousry uses `3♠`. The exact rung is not uniform, but the hand
   type recurs independently.

These are findings about the reviewed sources, not claims that every expert
partnership agrees.

## Reconciliation with BBA and pons

### What BBA actually does

The local EPBot probe targets the exact semantic object even though it is not
a published human system note. BBA's Muiderberg overcall is exactly five in
the major plus a four-card-or-longer minor. Its sampled actor hands are
otherwise unconstrained, the strength bands are 10th–90th percentiles, and the
shares below describe the probe distribution rather than live conditional
frequencies. At `1NT (2♥) ?` BBA uses
([BBA counter-defense census](bba-1nt-counter-defense.md)):

| Call | Approximate share | Empirical meaning |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| `X` | 25.3% | Four or more spades, about 5–17 HCP; takeout showing the other major. |
| `2NT` | 25.0% | Plain Lebensohl; opener bids `3♣` on every probed hand. |
| `-` | 23.3% | Mainly weak. |
| `3♥` | 6.5% | Cue. |
| `3NT` | 6.2% | To play. |
| `2♠` | 2.6% | Natural, five or more spades. |
| `4♠` | 2.5% | Natural, six or more spades, to game. |
| `3♣` / `3♦` | about 2.2% each | Natural, five or more cards. |

The `(2♠)` lane is symmetric: `X` shows four or more hearts and the cue is
`3♠`. After `1NT (2♥) X -`, opener never passes in the probe: it bids `2♠`
with four or more spades and otherwise `3♦`
([BBA counter-defense census](bba-1nt-counter-defense.md)).

BBA is closest to Bobby Wolff's panel description—other-major `X` plus
Lebensohl—but its exact strength bands and forced opener action are empirical
EPBot facts, not meanings published by Wolff.

The probe also forced the same `2M` call once with Multi-Landy declared and
once with it undeclared. BBA's response distribution moved by only 1.5% over
`(2♥)` and 0.1% over `(2♠)`. Thus BBA supplies a useful plain-Lebensohl
baseline, but its own behavior is weak evidence that exploiting the unknown
minor on round one matters
([declaration sweep](bba-1nt-counter-defense.md#the-headline-bba-routes-on-the-declaration-but-essentially-only-over-2)).

### What pons currently does

Pons has no Muiderberg opponent-disclosure flag. It therefore reads
`(2♥)`/`(2♠)` as a natural named-major overcall and applies Cohen Transfer
Lebensohl, without consuming exact-five, the other-major cap, or the unknown
four-card minor
([campaign §N2](../one-notrump-competitive.md#n2--muiderberg-22-the-lane-today);
[disclosure state](../../src/bidding/agreements.rs);
[responder table](../../src/bidding/american/competition/rubensohl.rs)). The
default direct structure is:

| Overcall | Weak action | Three-level constructive ladder | `X` / `3NT` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `(2♥)` | `2♠` natural weak; `2NT` relay with 6+ HCP, at most 8 points and a 5+ unbid suit | `3♣`→diamonds, `3♦`→spades, `3♥` cue-Stayman, `3♠`→clubs | optional/cooperative `X` with 2–3 hearts and 8+ HCP; `3NT` has 10+ points, a heart stopper and at most 4 heart HCP under the trap-pass split |
| `(2♠)` | `2NT` relay on the same bounds, including a heart signoff at the three level | `3♣`→diamonds, `3♦`→hearts, `3♥`→clubs, `3♠` cue-Stayman | optional/cooperative `X` with 2–3 spades and 8+ HCP; `3NT` has 10+ points, a spade stopper and at most 4 spade HCP under the trap-pass split |

The current method is externally recognizable but not identical to one source:

- Its known-major classification agrees with Kokish–Kraft,
  Pahk–Zelevinsky, Dubois, Walker and Cohen.
- Its transfer-through-the-major ladder is closest to Pahk–Zelevinsky.
- Its optional double is a measured pons treatment, not the negative/takeout
  double in that source and not BBA's guaranteed other major
  ([`DoubleStyle`](../../src/bidding/american/competition/penalty_double.rs)).
- After the optional double, opener passes with three or more cards in their
  major, and also when short without a five-card escape; with two or fewer and
  a five-card side suit, opener runs to it. In the probed `(2♥)` lane, BBA
  instead always bids `2♠` or its empirically observed `3♦` fallback.
- Pons spends the top available transfer rung on long clubs. Several richer
  external methods above repeatedly spend a high rung on both minors. That is
  a concrete candidate seam, not evidence that pons is wrong.
- Pons's 2026-08-21 response re-read found `X` positive, while the weak `2NT`
  relay and very weak passes were the adverse buckets. In particular, the
  class with 5 HCP or less and a six-card-or-longer suit remained negative on
  both scorers. Literature cannot overturn those local measurements
  ([campaign §N2](../one-notrump-competitive.md#n2--muiderberg-22-the-lane-today)).

The main continuations when the opponents pass are already authored. Opener
forces `3♣` after the weak `2NT` relay, after which responder passes or signs
off. After a major transfer opener bids game with three-card support and
otherwise `3NT`; after a diamond transfer opener chooses `3NT`, `3♦`, or the
finite Pass fallback. Opener answers the cue with the other major or `3NT`,
and answers the top-step club transfer with stopped `3NT` or `5♣`.

The current lane census pools NV and vulnerable boards from arms `053c4fb8`,
seed `1787064872`, 204,800 boards per vulnerability (run 2026-08-23):

| Lane | Boards | Plain | Plain/board | Perfect-defense, NV / vulnerable |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `(2♥)` | 409 | −99 | −0.24 | +0.20 / +0.84 |
| `(2♠)` | 402 | −90 | −0.22 | +0.18 / +0.48 |

Within the `(2♠)` lane, `X` scored +86 plain / +63 perfect-defense on 62
boards, while the `2NT` relay scored −32 / −68 on 45. The worst hand class was
5 HCP or less with a six-card-or-longer suit: 25 boards, −77 / −52. These are
diagnostic buckets, not an A/B of a replacement method.

Two broader A/Bs constrain any Muiderberg proposal but do not decide it. In
the fresh isolated comparison pooled across natural `(2♦)`/`(2♥)`/`(2♠)`
interference, current Transfer Lebensohl beat Plain on plain DD by
+0.30/+0.55 IMPs per divergent board NV/vulnerable
(+0.002/+0.003–0.004 per board, two seeds, 0.6% fire;
systems-on `2♣` excluded). The earlier perfect-defense revalidation also
favored Transfer, +0.46/+0.69 per divergent, but is historical rather than the
fresh isolation gate. Optional `X` beat Penalty by +1.59 and Takeout by +2.14
IMPs per divergent board. Therefore neither BBA's plain relay structure nor
its other-major double should be installed merely for alignment; each needs an
exact-lane experiment against the measured defaults
([option index](../bidding-options.md#a3--our-1nt-competition-runouts--escapes);
[perfect-defense revalidation](21gf-ledger.md);
[`DoubleStyle`](../../src/bidding/american/competition/penalty_double.rs)).

The authored first round also has real gaps: direct four-level responses are
already N7 queue work, and the natural-major lane has no continuation over
`1NT (2M) 2NT (X)`. Deeper interference after a transfer or cue is deliberately
floor-owned, not automatically missing book code. Any candidate must audit
those floor-owned tails and author a node only where the measured floor fails.

## Fact/inference boundary and supported candidates

### Facts established by sources

- A bid-major-plus-unknown-minor call is repeatedly treated as a known-major
  anchor, unlike a call showing two known suits.
- Standard Lebensohl, transfer Lebensohl, Rubensohl and a bespoke
  Kokish–Kraft structure are all documented against the target semantics.
- `X` has no expert consensus.
- The known major, not the unknown minor, controls first-round stopper logic.
- Both-minor responder hands receive an explicit call in several rich methods.
- No reviewed source provides a complete hidden-minor competitive tree.
- No external literature source compares these packages by controlled IMP
  measurement.

### Design inferences, not sourced agreements

The evidence makes the following packages authorable, but does not recommend
shipping any of them:

1. **BBA-alignment package:** plain Lebensohl with `X` guaranteeing four or
   more in the other major, plus BBA's measured opener response. This is
   front-end empirical mimicry of the exact target, not a complete anchor
   specification or published human consensus.
2. **Pahk–Zelevinsky package:** convertible takeout `X`, weak/slow `2NT`,
   `3♣`→diamonds, `3♦`→other major, cue-Stayman, `3OM` both minors, and their
   explicit five-five game-forcing calls. This is the cleanest full external
   first round.
3. **Kantar package:** stopper-showing Stayman `X`, transfers from `2NT`, a
   direct both-minors cue, slow stopperless Stayman/`3NT`, and a trap-pass
   reopening double. It is the cleanest expert-authored exact table with a
   double philosophy unlike both BBA and current pons.
4. **BWS penalty-process package:** ordinary anchor-suit Lebensohl, but use
   `X` to suggest penalizing the eventual escape and retain BWS's
   seat-specific delayed doubles. This is an expert-consensus baseline, not a
   finite implementation spec.
5. **Kokish–Kraft package:** negative `X`, neutral pass, asymmetric minor
   transfers, special three-suit shapes, both-minor calls and explicit jumps.
   Strength thresholds must be rebased for a strong 1NT and the ambiguous
   minor continuation resolved before implementation.
6. **Minimal pons package:** keep the current natural-major fallback, weak
   actions, transfers and stopper discipline, while testing double and
   clubs-versus-both-minors allocation as separate knobs. This is a synthesis
   for low-memory measurement, not a quoted external method.

Before any candidate is implementable, it needs explicit answers to:

- Does `X` promise the other major, general values, takeout shape, optional
  trump tolerance, a penalty hand, or Stayman with a stopper?
- What exactly must opener do after `1NT (2M) X -`?
- When responder passes with a penalty hand, when must opener reopen?
- What are `X` and cues after the opponents ask for or reveal the minor?
- What happens when they interfere over our `2NT` relay or transfer?
- Which call shows both minors, and what happens to the long-club hand that
  currently owns that rung?
- Does direct `3NT` show, deny, or merely not discuss a known-major stopper?
- What are the direct four-level calls, and which continuations survive
  competition?

Under the repo's measurement rule, each complete package still needs a seeded
A/B on both scorers. The immediate measured queue item is the existing N2d
trial: lower the weak relay floor for the 5-HCP-or-less, six-card-suit class
over `(2♠)` and compare it directly with Pass. Current optional `X` is already
positive in the lane and wins its broader A/B, so literature variants of `X`
are lower priority. Before building a both-minors treatment, first census the
reach of 5–5 minor hands and the calls displaced from the current club rung.
Only a material reachable class earns that A/B
([campaign queue](../one-notrump-competitive.md#package-queue--open-work-ranked-by-the-census)).

A Muiderberg disclosure/read-side change is a separate experiment from any of
those response tables. Today the reader does not consume exact-five or the
unknown minor; adding that information could change floor calls even with the
book byte-identical. Do not bundle the new reading with a new counter-defense:
first measure routing/reading against the current table, then measure each
response package on the same declared routing
([reading-drift handoff](../reading-drift-handoff.md)).
