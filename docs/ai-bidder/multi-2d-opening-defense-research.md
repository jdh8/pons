# Defending a Multi 2♦ opening — source survey

**Research date: 2026-08-25.** This document is about an opponent's Multi
**opening** `(2♦)`, normally a weak two in an undisclosed major
([ACBL defense](https://web2.acbl.org/defensedatabase/mc20.pdf)). It is not about
`1NT (2♦)`, the Multi-Landy/Woolsey overcall; that is a different auction and a
different convention.

Auctions are written from the defenders' point of view: opponents' non-pass
calls are parenthesised and every pass is `-`. `M` means either major, `m` either
minor, and `OM` the other major.

## Executive findings

There is no portable meaning for any of the three cheapest calls:

- Direct `X` is variously takeout of spades, a limited balanced or very strong
  hand, a major one-suiter, hearts or strong, or a multi-way transfer/takeout
  relay. All five allocations occur in federation defenses, filed partnership
  methods, or a named author's published method:
  [ACBL options one and two](https://web2.acbl.org/defensedatabase/mc20.pdf),
  [Todd–Fisher](https://cbf.ca/OnlineTeams/2023-2024/D1-1_Multi-vs-Multi.pdf),
  [Ginsberg–Travis](https://abfevents.com.au/events/playoffs/2016/SW/cc/Womens_Ginsberg_Travis.pdf),
  and [John Miller's authored method](https://bridgewinners.com/article/view/a-modern-defense-to-multi-2d/).
- Direct `2NT` is natural in most of the sourced methods, but the documented
  ranges include 15–19, 16–18, 17–19, and 19–21; in one filed transfer defense it
  is merely labelled natural, with no range on the card. See
  [Todd–Fisher](https://cbf.ca/OnlineTeams/2023-2024/D1-1_Multi-vs-Multi.pdf),
  [ACBL option two](https://web2.acbl.org/defensedatabase/mc20.pdf),
  [Nickell–Katz](https://usbf.org/docs/2025usbc/acblcards/KatzNickellWBF2022.pdf),
  [Miller](https://bridgewinners.com/article/view/a-modern-defense-to-multi-2d/),
  and [Ginsberg–Travis](https://abfevents.com.au/events/playoffs/2016/SW/cc/Womens_Ginsberg_Travis.pdf).
  After a direct double, however, advancer's `2NT` is often an artificial
  Lebensohl/puppet call, not notrump
  ([ACBL option two](https://web2.acbl.org/defensedatabase/mc20.pdf),
  [Nickell–Katz](https://usbf.org/docs/2025usbc/acblcards/KatzNickellWBF2022.pdf),
  [Rosen](https://s3-eu-west-1.amazonaws.com/ebumagazine/2013-04.pdf)).
- Direct `4m` is often a Leaping-Michaels-like minor-plus-major call, but its
  strength and suit partition are not uniform. ACBL uses either minor plus an
  unspecified major; Nickell–Katz put hearts-plus-minor at `4m` but
  spades-plus-minor at `3♠`; Miller instead uses `4♣` for both minors and `4♦`
  for a strong four-of-a-major hand. See the
  [ACBL defense](https://web2.acbl.org/defensedatabase/mc20.pdf),
  [Nickell–Katz notes](https://usbf.org/docs/2025usbc/acblcards/KatzNickellWBF2022.pdf),
  and [Miller notes](https://bridgewinners.com/article/view/a-modern-defense-to-multi-2d/).
- Passing first and doubling once a major appears is common, but `X` in
  `(2♦) - (2M) X`, `(2♦) - (2♥) - (2♠) X`, and
  `(2♦) X (2M) - - X` need not have the same strength or purpose. Complete
  defenses spell out all three; a rule merely saying "delayed doubles are
  takeout" is underspecified. See
  [Dixon's skeleton](https://db.worldbridge.org/bulletin/11_2%20Veldhoven/Veldhoven.2011.Bulletin.%5B12%5D.pdf),
  [ACBL option two](https://web2.acbl.org/defensedatabase/mc20.pdf), and
  [Nickell–Katz](https://usbf.org/docs/2025usbc/acblcards/KatzNickellWBF2022.pdf).
- The counter-defense matters. Multi responder may pass `(2♦)` or use
  pass/redouble as a diamond escape after `X`, raise pass-or-correct aggressively,
  or conceal the major for another round. The official ACBL defense therefore
  has separate advances after `(2♦) X (2M)`, after responder's pass
  `(2♦) X -` or redouble `(2♦) X (XX)`, after a three-level response, and
  after `(2♦) - -`; Nickell–Katz likewise enumerate those branches. See the
  [ACBL defense](https://web2.acbl.org/defensedatabase/mc20.pdf) and
  [Nickell–Katz notes](https://usbf.org/docs/2025usbc/acblcards/KatzNickellWBF2022.pdf).

The shortest sourced family is the ranged-double/Dixon family: natural suit
calls, `X` for a limited balanced hand or a strong hand, natural `2NT`, and pass
then takeout-double once their major is known
([Dixon's first-person outline](https://db.worldbridge.org/bulletin/11_2%20Veldhoven/Veldhoven.2011.Bulletin.%5B12%5D.pdf)).
It is simple, not complete by itself: the passed-out `(2♦)` case and every
advance after their interference still need rules.

## What the sources establish — and what they do not

### Regulation is not a technical verdict

ACBL's defense database says that its convention-approval subcommittee approves
written defenses and that a complete proposal must cover initial actions,
advances in competition, actions after opening–pass–response, and delayed
actions. That is a completeness requirement, not a claim that either approved
option is best bridge
([ACBL administration page](https://www.acbl.org/administration/)).

The separate ACBL policy PDF describes written-defense and consultation rules
for the **2025 Summer NABC** and specified regional events, with changes dated
August 4, 2025
([2025 policy](https://web2.acbl.org/defenseDatabase/Multi_2d_defenses_policy.pdf)).
It is historical evidence for the provenance and table use of the defense, not
evidence of what is legal in every 2026 event. Current convention charts and the
event's conditions of contest control current legality.

## 1. ACBL option one: split the two takeout doubles

The defining choice is to encode both possible weak-two takeout doubles
immediately:

| direct call over `(2♦)` | documented meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `X` | Takeout of spades; advance as after a takeout double of a natural weak `2♠`, including Lebensohl if the partnership uses it. [ACBL option one](https://web2.acbl.org/defensedatabase/mc20.pdf) |
| `2♥` | Takeout of hearts; advance as after a takeout double of a natural weak `2♥`, including Lebensohl if used. [ACBL option one](https://web2.acbl.org/defensedatabase/mc20.pdf) |
| `2♠` / `3♥` | Natural; after either natural major overcall, advancer's other-major call is a cue bid and jumps are natural. [ACBL option one](https://web2.acbl.org/defensedatabase/mc20.pdf) |
| `2NT` | 16–18 balanced; systems on as after a `2NT` opening. [ACBL option one](https://web2.acbl.org/defensedatabase/mc20.pdf) |
| `3♣` / `3♦` | Natural. [ACBL option one](https://web2.acbl.org/defensedatabase/mc20.pdf) |
| `3♠` | Strong and natural; `4♥` by advancer is a cue bid. [ACBL option one](https://web2.acbl.org/defensedatabase/mc20.pdf) |
| `4♣` / `4♦` | At least 5–5 in the bid minor and an unspecified major; over `4♣`, `4♦` asks for the major, while over `4♦`, `4♥` is pass-or-correct. [ACBL option one](https://web2.acbl.org/defensedatabase/mc20.pdf) |

The official continuations distinguish four different situations:

- In fourth seat, `(2♦) - (2M) X` is takeout of the major just bid; a direct
  `2NT` there is 15–18 and a direct `3M` or `4M` is natural
  ([ACBL option one](https://web2.acbl.org/defensedatabase/mc20.pdf)).
- If the responder's `2♥` is passed and opener corrects, then
  `(2♦) - (2♥) - (2♠) X` is a light takeout double of spades
  ([ACBL option one](https://web2.acbl.org/defensedatabase/mc20.pdf)).
- If the direct defender used `2♥` as takeout of hearts and opener then bids
  spades, `(2♦) - (2♥) X (2♠) X` is **penalty** opposite the heart
  takeout; `3♥` is natural and `3♠` asks for a spade stopper
  ([ACBL option one](https://web2.acbl.org/defensedatabase/mc20.pdf)).
- If responder passes the opening, `(2♦) - -` is defended as a natural weak
  `2♦` opening, not as a revealed major
  ([ACBL option one](https://web2.acbl.org/defensedatabase/mc20.pdf)).

Henry–Henry use the same split but add strong hands to the cheapest call:
`X` is takeout of spades **or strong**, `2♥` is takeout of hearts, other calls
are natural, and weak Lebensohl applies
([2025 supplement, note 13](https://www.nzbridge.co.nz/user/inline/5875/HenryHenry%20Mixed%20Jan%202025%20supps.pdf)).
That extra strong option is material: it means advancer cannot treat every
double as a pure spade-takeout hand until intervenor clarifies.

## 2. Dixon / ACBL option two: ranged double, delayed takeout

This family keeps both two-level major calls natural and postpones a takeout
double until the opponents show which major they hold.

### Dixon's own skeleton

In a 2011 WBF championship bulletin, Chris Dixon writes that the EBU committee
chair asked him in about 1975 to devise and publish a recommended defense. His
retrospective "Dixon type" skeleton is:

| position | calls |
| --- | --- |
| Direct, `(2♦) ?` | `X` = balanced 13–15 or any 19+ hand; `2M` natural; `2NT` = balanced 16–18; `3m` natural. [Dixon, p. 24](https://db.worldbridge.org/bulletin/11_2%20Veldhoven/Veldhoven.2011.Bulletin.%5B12%5D.pdf) |
| Fourth seat, `(2♦) - (2M) ?` | `X` = takeout; `2NT` = balanced 16–18; suits natural. [Dixon, p. 24](https://db.worldbridge.org/bulletin/11_2%20Veldhoven/Veldhoven.2011.Bulletin.%5B12%5D.pdf) |
| Sixth seat, after `(2♦) - (2♥) - -` or `(2♦) - (2♥) - (2♠)` | `X` = takeout; `2NT` = strong and natural with shortage in the other major; suit calls show two-suiters. [Dixon, p. 24](https://db.worldbridge.org/bulletin/11_2%20Veldhoven/Veldhoven.2011.Bulletin.%5B12%5D.pdf) |

This is a primary source for the family's allocation, but only a skeleton. It
does not supply the advances and interference tails needed by a machine bidder.

### ACBL option two supplies a full interference tree

| direct call over `(2♦)` | documented meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `X` | 13–15 balanced or any strong hand. With takeout of only one major, pass and plan to double later; with a strong hand, double now because `(2♦)` can be passed. [ACBL option two](https://web2.acbl.org/defensedatabase/mc20.pdf) |
| `2♥` / `2♠` | Natural. Advancer's other-major call is a cue bid; a jump in the other major is natural. [ACBL option two](https://web2.acbl.org/defensedatabase/mc20.pdf) |
| `2NT` | 16–18 balanced; respond as to a `2NT` opening. [ACBL option two](https://web2.acbl.org/defensedatabase/mc20.pdf) |
| `3♣` / `3♦` | Natural. [ACBL option two](https://web2.acbl.org/defensedatabase/mc20.pdf) |
| `3♥` / `3♠` | Strong and natural; advancer's other-major call is a cue bid. [ACBL option two](https://web2.acbl.org/defensedatabase/mc20.pdf) |
| `4♣` / `4♦` | At least 5–5 in the bid minor and an unspecified major, with the same `4♦` ask over `4♣` and `4♥` pass-or-correct over `4♦` as option one. [ACBL option two](https://web2.acbl.org/defensedatabase/mc20.pdf) |

### Once their major appears

- `(2♦) - (2M) X` and `(2♦) - (2M) - - X` are takeout of the bid
  major; normal weak-two-double advances, including Lebensohl if used, apply
  ([ACBL option two](https://web2.acbl.org/defensedatabase/mc20.pdf)).
- `(2♦) - (2♥) - (2♠) X` is takeout of spades
  ([ACBL option two](https://web2.acbl.org/defensedatabase/mc20.pdf)).
- After `(2♦) - (2M)`, the official option labels suit bids natural, even
  `3M` and `4M`; `X` carries takeout
  ([ACBL option two](https://web2.acbl.org/defensedatabase/mc20.pdf)). This is
  not the same cue-bid treatment used after one's own direct natural major
  overcall.
- `(2♦) X (2M) - - X` is 18+, not a routine minimum reopening double
  ([ACBL option two](https://web2.acbl.org/defensedatabase/mc20.pdf)).

### Advancing the ranged double under interference

After `(2♦) X (2M)`, the 2015 ACBL document assigns:

- `X` = balanced, at least invitational; with game-forcing values it shows a
  stopper only in the major the opponents bid and does not deny a four-card
  major;
- `2NT` = puppet to `3♣`, either a signoff or a game force with a minor;
- `3♣` = game-forcing Stayman, `3♦`/`3♥` = at-least-invitational
  transfers, `3♠` = both minors game-forcing, and `3NT` = 11–15 with both
  majors stopped.

All of those meanings come from
[ACBL option two](https://web2.acbl.org/defensedatabase/mc20.pdf). After the
`2NT` puppet, doubler's `3♣` shows the 13–15 balanced type; advancer may then
pass or bid `3♦` to play, `3♥` with game-forcing clubs, `3♠` with
game-forcing diamonds, or `3NT` with a game-forcing balanced hand that stops
only the unbid major. Instead of accepting the puppet, doubler's natural
`3♦`/`3♥`/`3♠` is 18+ and game-forcing, while `3NT` is natural 18–23
([ACBL option two](https://web2.acbl.org/defensedatabase/mc20.pdf)).

If Multi responder instead passes or redoubles, tending to show diamonds, the
same source gives a separate table: `2M` is to play, `2NT` starts the club
route, `3♣` is game-forcing Stayman, `3♦`/`3♥` are transfers,
`3♠` shows a game force without a diamond stopper, and `3NT` is natural
with 11–15 and both majors stopped
([ACBL option two](https://web2.acbl.org/defensedatabase/mc20.pdf)). This is why
"systems on after `X`" is not enough: the opponent's escape call changes the
strain one must stop.

### A real source-version disagreement

The 2015 ACBL database document makes advancer's double in
`(2♦) X (3x) X` responsive/"cards", makes a suit one-round forcing, and uses
`4x` as a cue bid
([ACBL 2015](https://web2.acbl.org/defensedatabase/mc20.pdf)). The older CBF
mirror instead labels that double penalty
([ACBL text mirrored by CBF, 2012](https://cbf.ca/files/Documents/BridgeWeek_docs/ACBL%20Defense%20to%20MULTI%20_2012_.pdf)).
Sokolow–Seamon-Molson's 2016 filed notes also use penalty there
([note 35](https://info.ecatsbridge.com/Systems/2016worldchampionships-wroclaw/womensteams/usa/sokolow-seamonmolson.pdf)).
The difference must remain explicit; silently merging the documents would
invent a method neither source states.

Nickell–Katz show how large a fully enumerated version can become. Their direct
calls include natural `2M`, natural `3♣`/`3♦`, intermediate `3♥`,
`3♠` as spades plus a minor game-forcing, `4m` as that minor plus hearts
game-forcing, and `4NT` as both minors
([note 9](https://usbf.org/docs/2025usbc/acblcards/KatzNickellWBF2022.pdf)).
Their notes then distinguish pass-or-correct interference, pass/redouble diamond
escapes, every three-level response, direct `2NT` inquiry, and a responder pass
of `(2♦)`. This is useful as a seam inventory, not a requirement to copy their
entire partnership system.

## 3. Transfer and "Multi versus Multi" families

These methods use ambiguity of their own instead of waiting for opener's major
to become known.

### Todd–Fisher: `X` is the undisclosed major

| direct call over `(2♦)` | documented meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `X` | A good five-card or any six-card major, with a hand willing to act at the two level. Advancer's major calls are pass-or-correct; `2NT` commits to game; `3m` is constructive. [Todd–Fisher](https://cbf.ca/OnlineTeams/2023-2024/D1-1_Multi-vs-Multi.pdf) |
| `2♥` | A good 15+ hand, usually six-plus clubs. [Todd–Fisher](https://cbf.ca/OnlineTeams/2023-2024/D1-1_Multi-vs-Multi.pdf) |
| `2♠` | A good 15+ hand, usually six-plus diamonds. [Todd–Fisher](https://cbf.ca/OnlineTeams/2023-2024/D1-1_Multi-vs-Multi.pdf) |
| `2NT` | 15–19 strong notrump with both majors stopped; systems on. [Todd–Fisher](https://cbf.ca/OnlineTeams/2023-2024/D1-1_Multi-vs-Multi.pdf) |
| `3m` | Natural, non-forcing, typically 9–14 with a six-plus-card suit. [Todd–Fisher](https://cbf.ca/OnlineTeams/2023-2024/D1-1_Multi-vs-Multi.pdf) |
| `3M` | Strong one-suiter with about eight-and-a-half to nine tricks. [Todd–Fisher](https://cbf.ca/OnlineTeams/2023-2024/D1-1_Multi-vs-Multi.pdf) |
| `4m` | Leaping Michaels. Over `4♣`, `4♦` asks for the major; over `4♦`, `4♥` or `4♠` is pass-or-correct. [Todd–Fisher](https://cbf.ca/OnlineTeams/2023-2024/D1-1_Multi-vs-Multi.pdf) |

This method still uses delayed takeout: pass with takeout of only one major and
double later, or pass with 12–14 balanced and bid notrump later if possible
([Todd–Fisher](https://cbf.ca/OnlineTeams/2023-2024/D1-1_Multi-vs-Multi.pdf)).
After `(2♦) X (2M)`, advancer's `X` is takeout of the bid suit and promises
at least three in the other major, `2NT` is natural 9–11, and `3m` is natural
and forcing
([Todd–Fisher](https://cbf.ca/OnlineTeams/2023-2024/D1-1_Multi-vs-Multi.pdf)).
After `(2♦) - (2NT)`, a double shows cards, usually 16+ balanced rather than
a trump stack; with takeout of only one major, advancer may pass and reopen
later
([Todd–Fisher](https://cbf.ca/OnlineTeams/2023-2024/D1-1_Multi-vs-Multi.pdf)).

### Miller: pair an overcall with takeout of the same major

Miller's cheapest calls each contain both a natural major hand and the hand
that wants to take that major out:

- `X` = a heart overcall, takeout of hearts, four spades with longer diamonds,
  15–18 balanced, or 22+ balanced;
- `2♥` = a spade overcall, takeout of spades, or four hearts with longer
  diamonds;
- `2♠` = a one-suited club overcall denying a four-card major;
- `2NT` = 19–21 balanced.

Those meanings are the author's own published table
([Miller, direct actions](https://bridgewinners.com/article/view/a-modern-defense-to-multi-2d/)).
The higher allocation is also deliberately nonstandard: `3♣` = clubs plus a
four-card major, `3♦` = diamonds without a four-card major, `3♥` = spades
plus a minor, `3♠` = hearts plus a minor, `4♣` = both minors non-forcing,
and `4♦` = a stronger-than-`4M` major hand
([Miller, direct actions](https://bridgewinners.com/article/view/a-modern-defense-to-multi-2d/)).

The design advantage is coverage: either possible major overcall and either
possible major takeout begins immediately. The cost is a large clarification
tree. The author supplies it, but also says the defense was not extensively
road-tested
([Miller, caveat and continuations](https://bridgewinners.com/article/view/a-modern-defense-to-multi-2d/)).

### Ginsberg–Travis: a filed but deliberately terse transfer card

The ABF card states only `X` = hearts or strong, `2NT` = natural, other calls =
transfers, and `3M` = stopper ask
([Ginsberg–Travis card](https://abfevents.com.au/events/playoffs/2016/SW/cc/Womens_Ginsberg_Travis.pdf)).
The source does **not** identify which call transfers to which suit or give
ranges. Filling in a plausible transfer ladder would be invention, so this
survey does not do it.

## 4. A simpler strong-double baseline

New Zealand Bridge's intermediate lesson recommends good suit overcalls and
says a direct double has 16+ with a rebid. Advancer's `2NT` after that double is
Lebensohl-style with 0–7; a positive 8+ response makes intervenor's continuation
game-forcing. Once the opponents bid a suit after defender's pass or double,
subsequent doubles are takeout. A direct `2NT` overcall must stop both majors
([NZ Bridge teacher notes](https://www.nzbridge.co.nz/user/inline/3302/NZB%20Intermediate%2011%20-%20Teacher%20Notes.pdf)).

This baseline gives up the limited balanced use of `X` but makes the strong-hand
meaning shallow. It still owes exact natural-overcall ranges, the passed
`(2♦)` case, and high-level two-suiters before it is a complete machine system;
the teacher notes do not supply those details.

## 5. The five seams a pons defense must decide

### 5.1 What `X` says about the unknown major

The source families solve different problems:

1. **Split takeout:** `X` takes out spades and `2♥` takes out hearts
   ([ACBL option one](https://web2.acbl.org/defensedatabase/mc20.pdf)). It acts
   immediately on both shapes but spends the natural `2♥` overcall.
2. **Ranged double:** `X` shows a limited balanced or strong hand; takeout of
   one major passes first
   ([ACBL option two](https://web2.acbl.org/defensedatabase/mc20.pdf)). It keeps
   natural major overcalls but can lose the clarification round if responder
   passes `(2♦)`.
3. **Undisclosed major:** `X` shows one major and lets advancer use
   pass-or-correct
   ([Todd–Fisher](https://cbf.ca/OnlineTeams/2023-2024/D1-1_Multi-vs-Multi.pdf)).
4. **Hybrid transfer/takeout:** the cheapest call contains both a major
   overcall and takeout of that same major
   ([Miller](https://bridgewinners.com/article/view/a-modern-defense-to-multi-2d/)).
5. **Strong-only:** `X` promises a strong hand with a rebid
   ([NZ Bridge](https://www.nzbridge.co.nz/user/inline/3302/NZB%20Intermediate%2011%20-%20Teacher%20Notes.pdf)).

These are mutually incompatible readings, not stylistic tweaks to one common
double.

### 5.2 Direct `2NT` versus advancer's `2NT`

The direct call `(2♦) 2NT` is 16–18 in both ACBL options, 17–19 for
Nickell–Katz, 15–19 with both major stoppers for Todd–Fisher, and 19–21 in
Miller's hybrid
([ACBL](https://web2.acbl.org/defensedatabase/mc20.pdf),
[Nickell–Katz](https://usbf.org/docs/2025usbc/acblcards/KatzNickellWBF2022.pdf),
[Todd–Fisher](https://cbf.ca/OnlineTeams/2023-2024/D1-1_Multi-vs-Multi.pdf),
[Miller](https://bridgewinners.com/article/view/a-modern-defense-to-multi-2d/)).

By contrast, `(2♦) X (2M) 2NT` is an artificial puppet to `3♣` in ACBL
option two and Nickell–Katz, and a low-strength Lebensohl action in Rosen's
scheme
([ACBL](https://web2.acbl.org/defensedatabase/mc20.pdf),
[Nickell–Katz](https://usbf.org/docs/2025usbc/acblcards/KatzNickellWBF2022.pdf),
[Rosen](https://s3-eu-west-1.amazonaws.com/ebumagazine/2013-04.pdf)).
A rule keyed only to "after Multi" rather than the exact auction will confuse
the two calls.

### 5.3 Cue bids and Leaping Michaels

The artificial `2♦` opening is not itself a usable natural cue. The sources
therefore create cues only after somebody identifies a real major:

- After a direct natural major overcall in either ACBL option, advancer's other
  major is a cue bid; after defender first passes and Multi responder names a
  major in option two, bids are instead natural and `X` is takeout
  ([ACBL](https://web2.acbl.org/defensedatabase/mc20.pdf)).
- ACBL `4m` shows that minor plus an unspecified major; `4♦` asks after
  `4♣`, while `4♥` is pass-or-correct after `4♦`
  ([ACBL](https://web2.acbl.org/defensedatabase/mc20.pdf)). Todd–Fisher use the
  same ask shape but explicitly allow either `4♥` or `4♠` as
  pass-or-correct over `4♦`
  ([Todd–Fisher](https://cbf.ca/OnlineTeams/2023-2024/D1-1_Multi-vs-Multi.pdf)).
- Nickell–Katz separate the major: `3♠` shows spades plus a minor
  game-forcing, while `4m` shows the bid minor plus hearts game-forcing
  ([Nickell–Katz](https://usbf.org/docs/2025usbc/acblcards/KatzNickellWBF2022.pdf)).
- Ginsberg–Travis label direct `3M` as a stopper ask rather than a natural jump
  or two-suiter
  ([ABF card](https://abfevents.com.au/events/playoffs/2016/SW/cc/Womens_Ginsberg_Travis.pdf)).

Thus "cue" and "Leaping Michaels" must be attached to exact calls, not inherited
from defense to a natural weak `2♦`.

### 5.4 Delayed doubles are auction-specific

| auction | sourced meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `(2♦) - (2M) X` | Fourth-seat takeout of the major responder bid in both ACBL options. [ACBL](https://web2.acbl.org/defensedatabase/mc20.pdf) |
| `(2♦) - (2♥) - (2♠) X` | Takeout of spades after opener's correction. [ACBL](https://web2.acbl.org/defensedatabase/mc20.pdf) |
| `(2♦) X (2M) - - X` | 18+ in ACBL option two; the original ranged doubler is showing extras. [ACBL](https://web2.acbl.org/defensedatabase/mc20.pdf) |
| `(2♦) X (2♥) - (2♠) X` | 20+, cooperative takeout of the corrected spade call in the Nickell–Katz treatment. [Nickell–Katz](https://usbf.org/docs/2025usbc/acblcards/KatzNickellWBF2022.pdf) |
| `(2♦) - - X` | Treated as action over a natural weak `2♦` in both ACBL options; Nickell–Katz explicitly allow takeout of diamonds or a very strong hand. [ACBL](https://web2.acbl.org/defensedatabase/mc20.pdf), [Nickell–Katz](https://usbf.org/docs/2025usbc/acblcards/KatzNickellWBF2022.pdf) |

The third row is the trap: using one generic delayed-takeout reader would turn a
strong repeat double into a minimum takeout double.

### 5.5 Counter-defense and interference tails

A complete defense needs at least these branches:

1. **They bid a pass-or-correct major after our `X`.** ACBL option two and
   Nickell–Katz give advancer artificial doubles, a `2NT` puppet, transfers,
   and stopper-sensitive notrump routes
   ([ACBL](https://web2.acbl.org/defensedatabase/mc20.pdf),
   [Nickell–Katz](https://usbf.org/docs/2025usbc/acblcards/KatzNickellWBF2022.pdf)).
2. **They pass or redouble to play diamonds.** ACBL option two changes the
   advance table and makes `3♠` the game force without a diamond stopper
   ([ACBL](https://web2.acbl.org/defensedatabase/mc20.pdf)).
3. **They jump to `3x` or `4M`.** The 2015 ACBL option uses responsive/cards
   doubles over `3x` and takeout over a `4M` response
   ([ACBL](https://web2.acbl.org/defensedatabase/mc20.pdf)); older filed methods
   can differ, as the Sokolow penalty double shows
   ([Sokolow–Seamon-Molson](https://info.ecatsbridge.com/Systems/2016worldchampionships-wroclaw/womensteams/usa/sokolow-seamonmolson.pdf)).
4. **They pass the opening.** Both ACBL options switch to defense against a
   natural weak `2♦`
   ([ACBL](https://web2.acbl.org/defensedatabase/mc20.pdf)); this branch cannot
   be recovered by waiting for a major that will never be named.
5. **Their double-response methods exploit our meaning.** Fleisher–Martel's
   actual Multi card uses pass over an opponent's double to ask opener to pass
   with three-plus diamonds and uses redouble to show responder's own suit;
   over a `2M`/`3M` overcall, responder's double is pass-or-correct
   ([2024 WBF card](https://mail.usbf.org/docs/2025usbc/acblcards/FleisherMartelWBF2024.pdf)).
   This is direct evidence that the diamond-escape and artificial-double tails
   occur in fielded opening methods, not just in the ACBL defense template.

## 6. Reconciliation with pons

### BBA's walked table is not a Multi defense

The existing walk records that BBA's `X` over their Multi is a generic takeout
double requiring three-plus cards in **every** suit, diamonds included; `2NT`
is 15–17, `3♦` and `4m` are natural, and the Michaels/Leaping-Michaels/stopper
hooks disappear because they are keyed to a natural diamond opening
([BBA walk](bba-multi-2d-opening.md)).
That direct table matches none of the sourced Multi-specific families above.
In particular it neither distinguishes takeout of hearts from takeout of
spades nor reserves `X` for a ranged/strong hand.

### The Dutch ledger's proposed repair is under-specified

The Dutch ledger correctly says that pons and BBA have no defense to a Multi
opening and proposes a "diamond-hooks-voided table"
([Dutch Phase 3 follow-up 2](../dutch-system.md#phase-3-follow-up-ledger)). The
online evidence says deleting diamond-specific hooks is necessary but not
sufficient. Authoring still has to choose:

- split-takeout, ranged-double/delayed-takeout, undisclosed-major, or hybrid
  meanings for `X` and `2♥`;
- an exact direct `2NT` range and a different exact meaning for advancer's
  `2NT` after `X`;
- the `4m` two-suiter mapping;
- every delayed-double strength;
- the responder-pass, pass/redouble-diamonds, pass-or-correct, and jump-response
  tails.

The Dutch opening is weak-only, 4–10 with exactly one six-card major
([Dutch Phase 3](../dutch-system.md#phase-3--the-multi-2-slice-in-two-variants)).
Therefore a defense need not protect against a strong **opener** option for this
specific system. A strong meaning inside defenders' `X` is still useful to
describe the defenders' own strong hands. Also, BBA's response table allows
responder to pass with six-plus diamonds
([BBA response table](bba-multi-2d-opening.md)), so the sourced
warning that `(2♦)` may be passed is live against the copied base, not merely a
historical full-Multi concern.

### Smallest complete prototype suggested by the sources

This is an architectural inference, not a measured bidding verdict: ACBL option
two/Rosen is the smallest fit with pons's existing natural weak-two defense.
Natural `2M`, `3m`, `3M`, and notrump machinery can stay natural; the new surface
is the ranged `X`, delayed takeout doubles, the direct/advancer `2NT` split, and
the interference tails. The split-takeout option needs an artificial `2♥`
package, while Todd–Fisher and Miller need larger pass-or-correct/transfer
compilers.

That is a candidate first A/B arm only. The official and expert sources contain
no IMP evidence that it will outperform the other families, and the repo's
measurement rules still decide whether any authored defense ships.

## 7. Authoring checklist extracted from the research

Before a defense is complete, record and test:

1. Which Multi variants are in scope: weak-only or strong options; five- or
   six-card major; whether responder may pass `(2♦)`.
2. Direct meanings for `X`, `2♥`, `2♠`, `2NT`, `3m`, `3M`, `3NT`, `4m`,
   `4M`, and `4NT`, including strength and suit length only where the selected
   source states them.
3. Advances after `(2♦) X -`, `(2♦) X (XX)`, `(2♦) X (2M)`,
   `(2♦) X (3x)`, and `(2♦) X (4M)`, including when pass or redouble is a
   diamond escape.
4. Fourth-seat action after `(2♦) - (2M)` and delayed action after
   `(2♦) - (2♥) - (2♠)`.
5. The meaning of a second double by the original doubler, separately from a
   first delayed double by a hand that initially passed.
6. Cue-bid and Leaping-Michaels mappings after a real major becomes known,
   including asks and pass-or-correct answers.
7. The passed-opening branch `(2♦) - -`.
8. Alert/readings for every artificial defensive call and finite catch-alls for
   every table; then a seeded A/B with both scorers before shipping.

The first seven items mirror the coverage categories ACBL itself requires for
a submitted defense—initial actions, responses under competition,
opening–pass–response auctions, and delayed actions
([ACBL administration](https://www.acbl.org/administration/)).

## Source limits

- This was a method survey, not an exhaustive history of named defenses. It
  prioritised federation documents and filed expert partnership notes over
  unattributed convention summaries.
- Dixon's 2011 WBF-bulletin article is a first-person retrospective, not the
  original 1970s publication; its regulation paragraph describes 2011, while
  its bidding table is used here as the author's method evidence.
- Terse cards were left terse. In particular, the Ginsberg–Travis transfer
  ladder and unlisted Fleisher–Martel continuations are not reconstructed.
- Point bands, vulnerability changes, and continuations were transcribed only
  when the cited source states them. Similar labels such as "Dixon",
  "Meckwell", "Leaping Michaels", and "Lebensohl" do not make two complete
  systems identical.
- No controlled performance data comparing these defenses was found. Any pons
  choice remains an authored convention change and therefore owes its own A/B.
