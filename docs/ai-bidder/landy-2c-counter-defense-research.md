# Counter-defenses to Landy `1NT (2♣)`

Research date: **2026-08-25**.

This is the companion to
[`multi-landy-2d-counter-defense-research.md`](multi-landy-2d-counter-defense-research.md).
It surveys methods for the opener's side after the exact auction
`1NT (2♣)`, where `(2♣)` is Landy and shows **both majors**, normally at
least 5-4. It is research, not a recommendation to change the shipped pons
system.

## Scope and source discipline

The meaning object matters more than the name. This paper includes a method
when its source explicitly applies to `2♣` Landy or to an artificial `2m` call
that shows both majors and explicitly names Landy as an example. It labels the
latter as **vehicle-independent**. It does not silently transpose any of these:

- Multi-Landy `1NT (2♦)`, where the long major is initially unknown;
- a Multi `2♦` opening;
- Cappelletti/DONT calls whose actual meaning is a one-suiter or a
  major-minor two-suiter; or
- generic advice after a natural `2♣` overcall.

Sources are ranked in this order: named partnership/system notes and authored
expert methods; filed federation cards; then teaching notes and expert-panel
or forum evidence. A filed card is strong evidence that a treatment was
actually declared, but its one-line disclosure is not evidence for omitted
continuations. No source below supplies a complete competitive tree after the
advancer doubles, raises, passes, or bids a major.

Auctions are normalized to pons notation: every pass is `-`, and only
opponents' non-pass calls are parenthesized. `Responder` is the partner of the
1NT opener; `overcaller` made `(2♣)`; `advancer` is overcaller's partner.
French abbreviations are translated as `H` = HCP, `REG` = balanced, `FM` =
game-forcing, `IC` = slam interest, *tenue* = stopper, and *chicane* = void.
Translations and source ambiguities are called out rather than repaired.

Opening ranges are part of the method. Kokish-Kraft, Gilles-Roupoil, and
Doub-Wildavsky play weak notrumps; *Système Jean Christophe* plays 15-16;
Incision varies from 13-15 to 17-19 by seat. Their point thresholds therefore
do not transfer mechanically to pons's 15-17 opening.

## Result in brief

There is no standard counter-Landy defense. The sources agree on the problem
and disagree on nearly every scarce call:

1. **Direct double is usually penalty-oriented or card-showing**, but its
   floor and its force differ. Published methods range from an exact 7-8 HCP
   proposal to an unlimited values action. BBA is unusual in that no double
   appeared in the finite probes reviewed here.
2. **Delayed double is not a generic fallback.** Cohen and Gilles-Roupoil make
   a pass-then-double takeout-ish; Kokish-Kraft makes it penalty; Kaplan makes
   it negative but passable by opener with four trumps; McCallum-Sanborn calls
   it cooperative. These cannot be combined by slogan.
3. **`2♦` is often a natural weak/competitive escape**, but several of the
   strongest exact methods simply omit it. An omitted `2♦` is a research gap,
   not permission to import the natural treatment.
4. **`2♥` and `2♠` have three incompatible jobs:** transfers or game forces in
   the minors; named-major stopper bids; or both-minor takeouts graded by
   strength/shape. Pons uses a fourth, closely related encoding: the bid major
   names a doubleton in a game-forcing both-minor hand.
5. **`2NT` is the main fault line.** It can be natural/invitational, a
   Lebensohl relay, a transfer to clubs, or a two-way minor action. A package's
   stopper and strength continuations depend on that choice.
6. **Direct `3NT` has mutually exclusive stopper meanings:** both majors
   stopped, neither stopped, no promise either way, or deliberately bid even
   with no stopper. The call cannot be copied without its whole relay logic.
7. **Only Kokish-Kraft assigns every four-level suit call.** Nearly all other
   sources stop at `3NT` or `3♠`; even Kokish-Kraft omits `4NT`. Their silence
   is material for a book implementation.

The main source families are:

| Family | Direct `X` | Low-call economy | Stopper route | Representative source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lebensohl + minor forces | can penalize a major | `2♥`/`2♠` show GF clubs/diamonds; `2NT` relays | slow route distinguishes one/both stoppers; fast `3NT` denies both | [Larry Cohen](https://www.larryco.com/bridge-articles/notrump-interference-advanced) |
| Kaplan stopper cues | can penalize a major | `2M` shows stopper; `2NT` natural/minor-oriented | direct cue names the stopped major; `3NT` neutral | [Doub-Wildavsky](https://www.tameware.com/adam/bridge/system/Doub-Wildavsky-static/index.html) |
| Full two-known-suits table | cards, opener may convert | separate competitive, invitational, GF, and splinter minor rungs | `2NT` branch and later stopper shows | [Kokish-Kraft](https://bridgewithdan.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/WEAK-NOTRUMP-SYSTEM-Kokish-Kraft-Jan-2008.pdf), p.161 |
| Rubensohl + singleton major | tightly ranged penalty proposal | `2M` identifies the sounder major; `2NT` transfers to clubs | opener may break the transfer to show stopper state | [Alain Lévy method](https://www.bridge-chailley.fr/wp-content/uploads/2013/03/E4038-D%C3%A9fense-contre-Landy-s.pdf) |
| Transfer/shape table | penalty process | minor transfers plus one- and two-major shortness encodings | varies; direct `3NT` often has no stopper promise | [Jean Christophe](https://www.clairebridge.com/textes/jean_christophe.pdf), p.82; [Incision](https://bridgewithdan.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Incision.pdf), p.29 |

## Exact-object methods

### 1. Larry Cohen: penalty double, minor game forces, and Lebensohl

Cohen's authored article has an explicit block for an opposing call below
`2♥` that shows both majors, and later gives `1NT (2♣=majors) X` as its Landy
example. This is the cleanest source that joins a full first-round core to an
explicit direct/delayed-double distinction. The article is written for a
strong-notrump context but gives no exact range in this block.
[Source: Larry Cohen, “Notrump Interference — Advanced.”](https://www.larryco.com/bridge-articles/notrump-interference-advanced)

| Call | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `X` | penalty-oriented; able to double at least one shown major; establishes a force |
| `2♦` | **not specified** in the both-majors block |
| `2♥` | game-forcing clubs |
| `2♠` | game-forcing diamonds |
| `2NT` | Lebensohl relay to `3♣` |
| `3♣` / `3♦` | natural, invitational |
| `3♥` / `3♠` | game-forcing both-minor hand, singleton or void in the bid major |
| `3NT` | to play, neither major stopped |
| four-level calls | **not specified** in this block |

After `1NT (2♣) 2NT - 3♣ -`, responder passes with weak clubs or bids `3♦`
with weak diamonds. A continuation of `3♥` or `3♠` shows only the named major
stopped; `3NT` shows both majors stopped. Thus weak, invitational, and forcing
minor hands and all three relevant stopper states are distinguished.
[Cohen](https://www.larryco.com/bridge-articles/notrump-interference-advanced)

Cohen says that a hand which passes directly and later doubles the opponents'
chosen `2M` is light takeout, not penalty. The article does not print all
intervening passes or say exactly how opener acts if advancer passes the
artificial `2♣`; those seat-by-seat branches remain unfilled. Its later
“front-of-card” four-level rule is expressly in the block for interference
that does **not** show both majors, so importing it here would be an inference.

### 2. Doub-Wildavsky's Kaplan defense: direct penalty, delayed negative

Doug Doub and Adam Wildavsky's live partnership notes explicitly say this
method applies when opponents bid an artificial `2m` for both majors, naming
Landy, Ripstra, and Cappelletti. This is exact for the Landy meaning object and
vehicle-independent about whether the call is `2♣` or `2♦`. Their base 1NT is
12-14, an important portability warning.
[Source: Doub-Wildavsky system notes, §C.17.b.vii.](https://www.tameware.com/adam/bridge/system/Doub-Wildavsky-static/index.html)

| Call | Kaplan meaning in the notes |
| --- | --- |
| `X` | wants to penalize at least one major |
| `-`, then a later `X` | negative; opener passes with four trumps; may also conceal a good balanced hand |
| opener's `2NT` pull of that delayed `X` | natural |
| `2♦` | **not specified** |
| `2♥` / `2♠` | stopper in the bid major, invitational or better |
| `2NT` | natural and minor-oriented, inferred from responder's failure to seek a penalty |
| `3♣` / `3♦` | competitive, limited by failure to double or cue |
| `3♥` / `3♠` | splinter; can be a 6-3 minor pattern |
| `3NT` | to play; neither promises nor denies major stoppers |
| four-level calls | **not specified** |

The same notes keep the balancing auction separate: after
`1NT - - (2♣)` Landy or Cappelletti, opener's double of the artificial minor
call is for business, while doubles of natural balancing bids are takeout.
That is valuable evidence against reusing the direct-seat table by call name
alone.
[Doub-Wildavsky, §C.17.d-e.](https://www.tameware.com/adam/bridge/system/Doub-Wildavsky-static/index.html)

### 3. Kokish-Kraft: the richest direct table

The Eric Kokish-Beverly Kraft weak-notrump notes print an exact table headed
`1NT (2C=S+H) LANDY`, also applying it to a Ripstra variant with the same two
known majors. It is the only located source that assigns every direct call
from `2♦` through `4♠` apart from `4NT`, as well as pass and double.
Its header says two known 4+ suits, so its target includes 4-4 Landy shapes,
broader than the usual 5-4+ promise in this paper's headline object.
[Source: Kokish-Kraft weak-notrump system, printed p.161.](https://bridgewithdan.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/WEAK-NOTRUMP-SYSTEM-Kokish-Kraft-Jan-2008.pdf)

| Call | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `-` | neutral; a later double is penalty |
| `X` | cards; invites opener to double a chosen major with four trumps, or three plus good defense |
| `2♦` | natural, competitive |
| `2♥` | both minors, competitive |
| `2♠` | both minors, invitational or better; show stoppers next |
| `2NT` | competitive with either minor, **or** balanced GF with a 5+ minor and one/both major stoppers |
| `3♣` | diamonds, GF, unbalanced |
| `3♦` | clubs, GF, unbalanced |
| `3♥` / `3♠` | both minors, GF, splinter in the bid major |
| `3NT` | natural, without full stoppers in both majors; with both, start `2NT` |
| `4♣` | Gerber |
| `4♦` / `4♥` | Texas |
| `4♠` | both minors, weak and very long |
| `4NT` | **not specified** |

The competitive `2NT` branch relays to `3♣`; responder passes with clubs or
corrects to `3♦`. On the balanced GF branch, the source says the next `3M`
shows that major stopped and `3NT` shows both. It does not enumerate how every
GF minor identity is recovered after those calls. After a direct GF transfer
at `3♣` or `3♦`, opener may show a stopper and responder can show a splinter
next, but the actual call table is not printed. These are real continuation
gaps despite the unusually complete first round.

The source filename says January 2008, while the PDF metadata says 2013 and
contains stale title metadata. The page headers consistently name
Kokish-Kraft; this paper therefore cites the partnership and page, not a
precise publication edition.

### 4. McCallum-Sanborn: strength-graded minor takeouts and a major one-suiter ladder

The McCallum-Sanborn notes give an exact section, “They bid Landy `2♣`,” and
say the same defense applies to other takeouts showing both majors. Their
two-level major calls are artificial negative-double substitutes focused on
the minors, normally 5-4 and presumed at least 4-4.
[Source: McCallum-Sanborn, section 805, printed pp.5-6.](https://www.fpbridge.pt/_wp/wp-content/uploads/2015/12/McCallun-defenses-against-opps-methods-Part-I.pdf)

| Action | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `X` | penalty-oriented in at least one major; strong desire to defend |
| `2♦` | **not specified** in section 805 |
| `2♥` | both-minor negative-double substitute, invitational or better |
| `2♠` | both-minor negative-double substitute, merely competitive |
| `2NT` | Lebensohl |
| direct `3♥` / `3♠` | natural major one-suiter, GF |
| direct `3NT` | both major stoppers |
| four-level calls | **not specified** |

The continuations carry much of the method:

- `2NT` followed by `3M` shows a stopper in the named major; it is not a
  natural invitation.
- `X` followed by `3M` is natural and invitational.
- A weak major one-suiter passes and bids later if appropriate; an
  invitational major starts with `X`; a non-defensive GF major bids `3M`
  immediately.
- Pass followed by double is cooperative. Pass followed by `2NT` is a hand
  that would have made a cooperative double of the other major and is often
  4-4 in the minors.

The page header says November 1999, but the text labels the `2♥`/`2♠` ordering
a July 2000 change. That internal chronology is odd but does not make the
printed call meanings ambiguous.

### 5. Alain Lévy: a tightly ranged penalty proposal and singleton-major Rubensohl

Olivier Chailley's one-page course attributes this method to Alain Lévy and
*Le Bridgeur*, June 2005. It is a teaching distillation rather than the
original article, so the attribution is stronger than the publication chain.
[Source: “Défense contre le Landy,” Lévy/Chailley.](https://www.bridge-chailley.fr/wp-content/uploads/2013/03/E4038-D%C3%A9fense-contre-Landy-s.pdf)

| Call | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `X` | exactly 7-8 HCP (8-9 total points), four cards in at least one major; penalty proposal |
| `2♦` | natural, competitive, rather weak |
| `2♥` / `2♠` | names responder's better major, implying a singleton in the other; exceptionally a small doubleton |
| `2NT` | transfer to clubs; positive hand with a six-card minor or both minors, normally short in a major |
| `3♣` | transfer to diamonds, practically GF |
| `3♦` / `3♥` / `3♠` | deliberately unassigned as too rare for the memory cost |
| `3NT` | balanced game values, about 9-10+ HCP, even with no major stopper |
| four-level calls | **not specified** |

After `X`, opener doubles the opponents' chosen major with four trumps. Opener
normally completes `1NT (2♣) 2NT - 3♣`, but with a maximum and a fit may break
to `3NT` with both major stoppers or to `3M` to show a problem in the other
major. After the normal `3♣` completion, responder's `3♦` shows both minors;
`3M` names the longer major and therefore shortness in the other; `3NT` shows
a diamond singleton.

The direct `2M` wording is unusual: it literally says “the better major,” not
“natural major,” and the example has only three cards in the bid major plus a
singleton in the other. This paper therefore does not translate it into a
natural suit bid or a formal stopper guarantee.

### 6. *Système Jean Christophe*: transfers, a hidden major, and a penalty process

This named public system plays a 15-16 notrump and gives a dedicated Landy
table on printed p.82.
[Source: *Système Jean Christophe*, version 16 July 2021, p.82.](https://www.clairebridge.com/textes/jean_christophe.pdf)

| Call | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `X` | 8+ HCP, balanced; begins a penalty process |
| `2♦` | natural, nonforcing; a free bid |
| `2♥` / `2♠` | GF minor two-suiter, 5-4 or 4-4, plus the source's unclear `singleton M4` condition |
| `2NT` | transfer to clubs, no precise strength |
| `3♣` | transfer to diamonds, GF; weaker diamonds use `2♦` |
| `3♦` | one five-card major, GF, especially with a singleton in the other major |
| `3♥` / `3♠` | 5-5 minors, void in the other major, slam interest |
| `3NT` | 9+ to 15 HCP, balanced, with or without major stoppers |
| direct pass / four-level calls | **not specified** |

After `1NT (2♣) 3♦ -`, opener relays `3♥`; responder bids `3♠` with five hearts
and `3NT` with five spades, keeping the 1NT opener declarer. The source's
`singleton M4` text in the `2M` row is not explained. A closely related French
table elsewhere uses “singleton in the other major,” but silently substituting
that wording here would conceal a source defect.

The immediately preceding Multi subsection defines a named *processus
punitif*: after the initial double, later actions can be a natural inferred
five-card major, another penalty double, a cue asking for a stopper, or `3NT`
with the stopper. The Landy row says it starts that same process, so this is a
reasonable cross-reference, but not a printed seat-by-seat Landy continuation.

### 7. Incision: a wide transfer/stopper table with seat-dependent NT ranges

Aviv Shahaf's Incision notes explicitly group Landy, Cappelletti, and Michaels
when `2♣` or `2♦` shows both majors. It is exact by meaning, but Incision opens
1NT with 13-15 in first/second seat and 17-19 in third/fourth, so the stated
5-9 bands are not pons-ready thresholds.
[Source: *Incision*, printed p.29.](https://bridgewithdan.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Incision.pdf)

| Call | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `-`, then `X` | competitive with both minors |
| `X` | penalty-oriented; if followed by `3m`, the minor is natural and GF |
| `2♦` | 5+ diamonds, 5-9 HCP |
| `2♥` | GF with heart stopper, seeking a spade stopper for `3NT` |
| `2♠` | GF with spade stopper, seeking a heart stopper for `3NT` |
| `2NT` | transfer to clubs, 6+ clubs and 5-9; may correct to diamonds if `2♦` was unavailable |
| `3♣` / `3♦` | natural, GF |
| `3♥` | GF, 5+ clubs and 4+ diamonds |
| `3♠` | GF, 5+ diamonds and 4+ clubs |
| `3NT` / four-level calls | **not specified** |

The source appends “or up to `4m`” to each `2M` stopper row without explaining
whether that changes strength, destination, or continuation. It should remain
an unresolved source phrase, not become a pons constraint.

### 8. Two simpler exact treatments

Sudhir Aggarwal recommends `X` penalty-oriented in one major, `2♦` natural to
play, `2♥` both minors invitational or better, `2♠` and `2NT` as transfers to
clubs and diamonds respectively, `3m` invitational with a six-card suit and
two of the top three honors, and `3M` natural forcing. Pass, `3NT`, four-level
calls, and continuations are absent.
[Source: Aggarwal, “Notrump Defense (Part 2),” p.1.](https://www.bridgewebs.com/dba/Notrump_Defense.pdf)

The Pattaya Bridge Club sheet gives a low-memory exact Multi-Landy lane:
direct pass is generally below invitation but may be a stronger waiting hand;
`X` can penalize at least one major; `3♣` is natural forcing; everything else
is basically natural/Lebensohl. It explicitly says Stayman is unnecessary once
both majors have been shown. That phrase does not define the direct `2♦`,
`2M`, `2NT`, `3NT`, or delayed-double continuations in enough detail to author
them without the club's separate Lebensohl agreements.
[Source: Pattaya Bridge Club, “Defence to Multi Landy.”](https://www.pattayabridge.com/conventions/Defense_MultiLandy.pdf)

## Further exact evidence, and why it remains partial

### French stopper and transfer variants

C. Merienne-C. Martel's 2025 partnership system uses `X` to begin a penalty
process, natural `2♦` to play, `2M` to show a stopper in the named major and
deny one in the other, `2NT`/`3♣` as transfers to clubs/diamonds,
`3♦`/`3♥` as transfers showing shortness in hearts/spades with a 5-4 minor
two-suiter, and `3♠` for 5-5 minors. It gives no direct `3NT`, four-level table,
or Landy-specific delayed/interference tail.
[Source: Merienne-Martel, *Système Claire Colette*, p.40.](https://www.clairebridge.com/textes/colette.pdf)

A one-page teaching sheet attributed to Bessis corroborates `X` with four
cards in a major, `2M` showing the named-major stopper, `2NT`/`3♣` transfers to
clubs/diamonds when neither major is stopped, `3M` as a 5-4 minor hand short in
the other major, and artificial `3♦` as game values with no major stopper. It
does not print `2♦`, `3NT`, the strength of `X`, or continuations, and is not
the original Bessis publication.
[Source: Amiens Bridge teaching sheet, “Défense contre le Landy (Bessis).”](https://www.amiens-bridge.fr/documents/defense-contre-le-landy-304081)

Gilles-Roupoil play Rubensohl over Landy: `X` is either penalty in at least one
major or an invitational hand planning `2NT`; `2M` is GF with the named-major
stopper and a problem in the other; pass then a bid is natural invitational.
The notes first call pass-then-double takeout, then later call it an
invitational hand with a stopper problem in the named suit. Those terse
statements may describe compatible hand types, but the auction is not printed
fully enough to prove that. Their 1NT is 11-14 in first through third seat
not vulnerable and 12-14 otherwise.
[Source: Gilles-Roupoil system v1.1, §2.3.2, printed p.7.](https://www.normalesup.org/~glafon/bridge/gilles.pdf)

## Expert evidence: no consensus on double

The direct call is less contentious than its continuations. A useful taxonomy
is:

| Source | Direct `X` | Later-double statement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cohen | can penalize at least one major; force | light takeout of chosen major |
| Doub-Wildavsky / Kaplan | wants to penalize at least one major | negative; opener converts with four trumps |
| Kokish-Kraft | cards; opener converts with four trumps or three plus defense | penalty |
| McCallum-Sanborn | penalty-oriented in at least one major | cooperative |
| Lévy | exactly 7-8 HCP and four in at least one major | not specified |
| Jean Christophe | 8+ HCP, balanced, starts penalty process | not specified in Landy subsection |
| Incision | penalty-oriented unless followed by natural GF `3m` | competitive both minors |
| EBU *Full Standard English* | general strength, about 10+ HCP | all later doubles penalty; the source is not seat-specific |
| BBA | no double appeared in these probes | no published delayed path from the probes |
| pons | values, 8+ HCP | unauthored/floor-owned |

Steve Robinson's expert panel reinforces that this is a partnership choice,
not settled theory. Henry Bethe calls double card-showing when `2♣` guarantees
both majors; Barry Rigal exempts pure artificial Landy from his otherwise
takeout first doubles; John Mohan proposes values plus transfer Lebensohl and
then penalty doubles; Nick Nickell wants to penalize one major and lets opener
judge. The same panel disagrees again in the balancing auction, with expert
answers ranging across takeout, cards, and penalty. Those are individual
testimonies, not one coherent system.
[Source: Steve Robinson, “Defense against notrump,” expert panel.](https://www.nvba.org/Robinson/articles/defense-against-notrump.html)

### The seat-by-seat decision that an implementation needs

The shorthand “pass then double” hides at least two different actors:

1. After `1NT (2♣) - (2M)`, **opener** acts immediately. Is opener's `X`
   penalty, takeout, or a compulsory reopening action?
2. If opener and overcaller pass, responder gets a second turn. What does
   responder's delayed `X` mean, and what may opener convert?
3. After `1NT (2♣) X (2M)`, does opener's second double show four trumps,
   three good trumps, or merely a maximum? Is pass forcing?
4. If advancer passes or doubles rather than selecting a major, how long does
   the force created by responder's `X` last?

Kokish-Kraft supplies an opener conversion threshold after the direct values
double. Kaplan supplies one after a delayed negative double. Cohen states that
the direct double creates a force. None prints all four branches. Treating one
source's missing branch as another source's continuation would build a hybrid
that no source actually plays.

#### Probed: what BBA does at item 3's seat (2026-08-28)

`probe-bba-constraints --mode opener-c-x2h|opener-c-x2s`, new modes, exact
twins of the Multi lane's `opener-d-x2h`/`opener-d-x2s`. Seat 0 over
`1NT (2♣) X (2M)`, default `--conv Multi-Landy=1` (the arm that makes their
`2♣` both majors), 100,000 hands per vulnerability filtered to the 5,005 that
BBA itself opens 1NT with, `--min-share 0.005`. The Multi twins are re-run at
the same size as the control.

**BBA reads the two doubles differently, and that is the headline.**
`epbot_get_info_meaning` over 200 hands, identical on all 200 in every cell:

| lane | their overcall | our `X` | their advance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `1NT (2♦)` Multi | “Multi-Landy, Multi” | **“negative double”** | “to the partner's longer” |
| `1NT (2♣)` Landy | “Multi-Landy, both majors” | **“bidable suit”** | “weak” |

Two findings, in the order they bind:

1. **BBA never doubles at this seat, in either lane** — no `X` bucket clears
   0.5% in any of the eight cells. That is the evidence behind the §N1l scope
   call that leaves opener's `X (2M)` seat unauthored, and it is the same
   evidence that refuted §N1k's authored `3NT` there.
2. **The Landy seat is *not* the Multi seat.** The expectation carried into the
   probe was 92–94% Pass on both legs, transplanted from the Multi twin; the
   Landy legs pass **67%** non-vulnerable and **80%** vulnerable, acting on a
   natural `3♣` with four-plus clubs the rest of the time. The vulnerability
   swing (30% → 18%) is real and one-directional. This does **not** move §N1l,
   whose ladder sits one call *later* and is reached only when our opener
   passes — but it does mean “opener sits at the doubler's seat” cannot be
   justified by pointing at the Multi lane. Recorded, not acted on; see the
   §N1l flag in [docs/one-notrump-competitive.md](../one-notrump-competitive.md).

## `2NT`, cues, `3NT`, and jumps

### `2NT`: four different calls sharing one label

| Meaning | Sources |
| --- | --- |
| Lebensohl relay to `3♣` | Cohen; McCallum-Sanborn; Pattaya in outline; Clifford-Finikiotis card |
| transfer to clubs | Lévy; Jean Christophe; Incision; Merienne-Martel; current pons |
| natural, minor-oriented | Doub-Wildavsky / Kaplan |
| competitive one-minor **or** balanced GF 5+ minor with stopper(s) | Kokish-Kraft |
| natural invitation | BBA |

The call therefore cannot be assessed alone. With Lebensohl, weak clubs and
diamonds are delayed and stopper state is shown after `3♣`. With a club
transfer, clubs are right-sided cheaply but natural 2NT disappears. In
Kokish-Kraft it is overloaded intentionally and depends on follow-up calls.
In BBA it remains a plain invitation while the minor transfers sit on `2♠`
and `3♣`.

### Major cue bids

The low major calls encode four distinct kinds of information:

- Cohen: the bid level maps to a **minor one-suiter** (`2♥` clubs, `2♠`
  diamonds), GF.
- Kaplan, Incision, Merienne-Martel, and the Bessis sheet: the bid major names
  a **stopper** in that major, with different strength and other-major
  implications.
- McCallum-Sanborn use both `2M` calls for **both minors**, grading strength;
  Aggarwal uses only `2♥` for both minors and spends `2♠` as a club transfer.
- Current pons: `2M` is a GF both-minor hand with exactly a doubleton in the
  **bid** major; `3M` is the corresponding 0-1 splinter.

This makes “cue bid” too imprecise for a rule name or reading. A call must say
whether it names a stopper, shortness, a target minor, or merely a strength
rung.

### `3NT`: the fast/slow inversion is source-specific

| Source | Direct `3NT` stopper statement |
| --- | --- |
| Cohen | neither major stopped; slow route shows one or both |
| McCallum-Sanborn | both majors stopped |
| Kokish-Kraft | lacks full stoppers in both; with both use `2NT` |
| Lévy | bid on balanced game values even with no major stopper |
| Kaplan | neither promises nor denies stoppers |
| Jean Christophe | balanced 9+ to 15, with or without stoppers |
| Incision | not specified |
| BBA | to play; probe does not establish a formal stopper condition |
| pons | high-priority row requires both stoppers/no six-card minor; lower-priority row is ungated on stoppers |

### Three- and four-level shape calls

Direct `3M` is a both-minor splinter in Cohen, Kokish-Kraft, Kaplan, the Bessis
sheet, and current pons; natural GF in McCallum-Sanborn; a strength/direction
minor call in Jean Christophe and Incision. This is another non-portable
surface match.

Kokish-Kraft alone specifies `4♣` Gerber, `4♦`/`4♥` Texas, and `4♠` as weak,
very long minors. BBA has a different direct `4♠`, discussed below. No source
located assigns direct `4NT`, and most do not say whether ordinary Texas or
Gerber survives. Those calls must be explicit in any candidate package.

## Reconciliation with BBA and pons

### What BBA actually does, including the rare call the old census missed

The existing BBA probe found this main ladder at `1NT (2♣) ?`: `3NT` 49.7%,
pass 26.8%, natural invitational `2NT` 13.6%, `6NT` 2.9%, `3♣` transfer to
diamonds 2.9%, `2♠` transfer to clubs 2.8%, and `7NT` 0.5%. Opener completes
both minor transfers 100%; no double appeared in that finite probe.
[Local evidence and reproduction commands.](bba-1nt-counter-defense.md#2-landy--a-notrump-ladder-no-double-minor-transfers-only)

A fresh `--min-share 0` probe on 2026-08-25 corrects one material claim in
that document: BBA also bids direct `4♠` on 178 of 40,000 hands
(**0.445%**), at both vulnerabilities. Its reported suit bands were 5-5 in
the minors and its candidate sketch was 5+ clubs and 5+ diamonds; the reported
HCP band was 3-17, median 12; 200/200 meaning labels say `Minors`. These are
percentile summaries, not proof that every raw hand was exactly 5-5. After
`1NT (2♣) 4♠ -`, among 1,992 sampled 15-17 openers BBA chose `5♣` 57.0%,
`5♦` 41.8%, and `4NT` 1.3%. This is a forcing both-minor placement request,
not a natural spade call.

The same min-share-zero run also emitted tiny direct natural-suit buckets that
the old inventory filtered out: `3♦` 85/40,000 (0.2%; reported 6-7 diamonds,
3-5 HCP) and `5♣`/`5♦` 21/40,000 each (0.1%); each returned `bidable suit` on
200/200 meaning probes. The `4♠` correction is the structurally important one
because it disproves the old “no both-minors call” claim.

Reproduce the direct probe with:

```text
# Same 178/40k with --vul both --min-share 0.003.
cargo run --quiet --release --example probe-bba-constraints -- \
  --mode custom --seat 0 --calls '1NT 2♣ 4♠' --samples 1 \
  --meanings 200 --min-share 0 --seed 20260825
cargo run --quiet --release --example probe-bba-constraints -- \
  --mode custom --seat 0 --calls '1NT 2♣ 4♠ -' --filter-call 1NT \
  --filter-prefix '' --vul none,both --samples 40000 --min-share 0 \
  --seed 20260825
```

The older “no both-minors call” summary in
[`bba-1nt-counter-defense.md`](bba-1nt-counter-defense.md) and the closed N1
archive is therefore stale and should be corrected separately. This research
task does not modify those files.

BBA's structure is not Cohen, Kaplan, or pons. It keeps the notrump ladder,
moves one-suited minors through `2♠`/`3♣`, showed no values double in these
probes, and spends a rare high call on both minors. Its declaration-sensitive
behavior remains important: when the forced `(2♣)` is not declared Landy,
BBA treats it as natural and its call distribution changes radically.

### Local discrepancies and reachable gaps found during this research

1. **The BBA inventory is stale:** the rare direct `4♠` both-minors request
   disproves the archived statement that BBA has no both-minor call. It does
   not make BBA equivalent to pons: BBA asks for placement at the four level,
   while pons devotes `2M`/`3M` to GF both-minor shape.
2. **A manufactured-club catch-all is reachable.** After a pons `2M` takeout,
   opener may choose notrump either with the short-major stopper **or** with no
   four-card minor. If responder then asks for the other-major stopper and
   opener lacks it, `landy_bba_ask_answer` ends with a `4♣` catch-all. On the
   no-minor branch that can manufacture `4♣` without four clubs. This is a
   live authoring gap, not a research recommendation to patch it silently.
3. **The exact-auction gate has a known sibling leak:** the repository notes
   that the same `their.two_clubs_landy` disclosure can misapply one suit lower
   after our 1NT overcall, for example `(1♦) 1NT (2♣)`. Clearing it changes the
   measured Landy base, so it remains flagged rather than silently fixed.
4. **Two campaign overview tables still describe the pre-N1 route.** The
   opponent-model table in [`one-notrump-competitive.md`](../one-notrump-competitive.md)
   still says pons treats `(2♣)` as natural clubs with a systems-on rebase, and
   its coverage summary still states the older shallow book/floor split. The
   live disclosure gate and N1 table above are authoritative.
5. **`TheirDisclosures` has stale rustdoc.** Its `two_clubs_landy` comment still
   describes the old natural-minor counter and a 5-4+ reading. The current
   first-round book is the BBA-ladder hybrid, while the live reader records
   4-4+ in the majors and makes no strength claim. This is documentation drift,
   not evidence that the live behavior should change.
6. **The BBA probe's documented percentile band is stale.** The BBA paper says
   reported bands are 10th-90th percentiles, but the CLI's current default
   `--trim 0.05` reports 5th-95th. Fresh probe output and code take precedence
   until that prose is corrected.
7. **One formal catch-all is unreachable.** In
   `landy_bba_takeout_answer`, `stopper_in(short) | no_minor` plus the two
   four-card-minor branches already cover every hand: if `no_minor` is false,
   at least one minor is four-plus. The source itself says the final notrump
   catch-all merely restates that union. Leave it for a separate byte-identity
   cleanup; this research task makes no code change.

The reversible default for all seven discrepancies is the same: preserve live,
measured behavior; make doc-only corrections separately unless a new A/B
authorizes a bidding change.

The current package shipped through the repository's dual-scorer measurement
gate. External systems are therefore candidate mechanisms to test, not reasons
to replace it by theory alone.

## Candidate coherent packages supported by the evidence

These are **research candidates, not ship recommendations**. “Coherent” means
the calls listed come from one source family and do not mix contradictory fast
and slow stopper meanings. It does not mean every competitive tail is ready to
author.

### Candidate A — source-exact Kokish-Kraft first round

Use the published p.161 direct table verbatim: neutral pass/delayed penalty
double; cards `X` with opener conversion; natural `2♦`; strength-graded
both-minor `2♥`/`2♠`; two-way `2NT`; GF minor transfers and splinters; the
published `3NT` and four-level calls.

Why it is a real candidate: it is the only located method assigning every
direct suit call from `2♦` through `4♠`, and it separates competitive,
invitational, GF, stopper, and splinter hands.

What must still be designed: direct `4NT`, exact weak-NT-to-15-17 strength
translation, the two-way `2NT` disambiguation, opener's stopper table after
`3m`, all advancer interference, and finite tails after the force. Any such
additions must be marked as pons synthesis rather than Kokish-Kraft.

### Candidate B — Cohen's Lebensohl/minor-force core

Keep the source-exact core: penalty-oriented `X`; delayed light-takeout double;
`2♥`/`2♠` GF clubs/diamonds; `2NT` Lebensohl; direct `3m` invitational; direct
`3M` both-minor splinter; fast `3NT` with neither stopper; slow one/both-stopper
routes.

Why it is a real candidate: it has the clearest complete strength and stopper
partition for the minors and an explicit double polarity.

Unresolved before authoring: Cohen omits direct `2♦` and the both-major
four-level block. Choosing natural weak `2♦` and an explicitly specified
four-level schedule would be **pons synthesis**, not Cohen's printed method;
his generic “front-of-card” paragraph belongs to the non-both-major branch and
cannot supply it. The force after `X` also needs exact opponent-action branches.

### Candidate C — Kaplan stopper/splinter core

Keep direct `X` penalty, delayed `X` negative/passable, `2M` as named stopper
INV+, natural minor-oriented `2NT`, competitive `3m`, splinter `3M`, and
stopper-neutral `3NT`.

Why it is a real candidate: it is a live named-partnership method, has the
best explicit direct-versus-delayed-double distinction, and resembles pons's
shape calls without changing the meaning of `3NT` by inference.

Unresolved before authoring: direct `2♦`, exact minor game forces, four-level
calls, the “good balanced” delayed-double branch, and every interfered tail.
The 12-14 base also makes strength translation mandatory.

### Candidate D — Incision's transfer/stopper table

Keep the complete printed run through `3♠`: direct penalty double with a GF
minor rebid exception, delayed competitive both-minor double, natural weak
`2♦`, stopper-seeking `2M`, weak club transfer, natural GF `3m`, and ordered GF
both-minor `3M`.

Why it is a real candidate: it assigns nearly every scarce call and directly
separates weak diamond/club escapes, stopper search, one-suited GF minors, and
both minors.

Unresolved before authoring: the unexplained `2M` parenthetical, direct `3NT`,
four-level calls, and recalibration across Incision's radically different
seat-dependent NT ranges.

### Candidate E — current pons as the measured control

Keep the shipped BBA-ladder hybrid unchanged as the control arm. A research
arm can then change **one coherent package**, including its readings and
interfered tails, rather than swapping isolated calls. The fresh BBA `4♠`
result suggests a separable probe — whether a high both-minor placement request
adds anything when pons already shows GF minors below `3NT` — but it is not a
reason to add the call unmeasured.

Every prototype remains subject to
[`measurement.md`](../measurement.md): same seed across arms, both plain DD and
perfect-defense scoring, trace the worst divergent boards, and complete both
sides' continuations before reading a verdict.

## Research gaps and authoring checklist

No located method answers all of these. A pons package must:

1. Pin the opponent's promise: 5-4+, 4-4 allowed, strength, seat, and
   vulnerability. Landy and Ripstra may share the two shown suits but not the
   same club inference.
2. Give `X` a strength/shape floor, say whether it creates a force, and specify
   opener's conversion threshold after each advancer action.
3. Spell separate meanings for opener's immediate double, responder's delayed
   double, and doubles after our artificial `2M`, `2NT`, or `3♣` is doubled.
4. Assign direct `-`, `2♦`, `2♥`, `2♠`, `2NT`, every `3x`, `3NT`, every `4x`,
   and `4NT`; source silence is not a finite catch-all.
5. Define weak, invitational, and GF paths for clubs, diamonds, both minors,
   and the rare natural major one-suiter.
6. Define all four stopper states: neither, hearts only, spades only, both.
   “Cue” must say whether it shows a stopper, asks for one, names shortness, or
   transfers to a minor.
7. Author advancer's pass-or-preference ladder after our `X`, including the
   case where advancer passes the artificial `2♣`, and continuations after the
   opponents redouble, raise, or compete to the four level.
8. Author forced completions and rebids after minor transfers, including
   interference before and after completion and a real landing spot when no
   major stopper exists.
9. Ensure every artificial call has an alert and an `Inferences` reading; do
   not let a bid-major shortness call read as a natural major.
10. Check the disclosure/seat gate so the counter fires only over our 1NT
    opening and the exact opponents' meaning, not over a natural `2♣` or after
    our 1NT overcall.
11. Measure the whole package against the shipped control. Right-siding and
    obstruction are not visible to plain double-dummy, so both repository
    scorers remain necessary.

The largest documentary gap is not the first-round table; it is the force and
penalty tree after the opponents choose a major. The next research pass would
benefit more from original partnership continuations or direct expert
clarification on those auctions than from another one-line convention card.
