# Counter-defenses to the Multi-Landy `1NT (2♦)` overcall

**Research date:** 2026-08-25. **Auction:** our side opens `1NT`; RHO overcalls
an artificial `2♦`; the
overcall shows one as-yet-unknown long major. This is **not** a Multi `2♦`
opening.

## Scope and source discipline

The target is the BBA/Woolsey lane: `1NT (2♦)` shows one long major, normally
six or more cards. David Oakley's published Multi-Landy sample describes the
overcall as a six-card-or-longer major below about 16 HCP, but its actual
defense chapter is outside the sample; the sample establishes only that Part 4
covers defenses and treats Lebensohl as basic, not any unseen call meanings
([Oakley sample, pp. 8–10 and contents pp. iii–v](https://masterpointpress.com/uploads/files/products/samples/731/SAMPLE_HONORS_MultiLandy2ndEdition.pdf)).

Source scope matters. Some documents also call `2♦` “Multi-Landy” when it can
be **any** one-suiter, including a minor. Such a method is evidence for a design
family, not evidence that the same calls are sound against BBA's pure-major
meaning. Pattaya's version is the clearest example: its `2♦` may be any suit,
although it says a major is usual
([Pattaya's Multi-Landy definition](https://www.pattayabridge.com/conventions/MultiLandy_main.htm)).

The source order used here is:

1. published partnership/system notes and official convention cards;
2. authored expert methods and expert-panel testimony;
3. club teaching notes;
4. forum discussion only as a taxonomy or a lead to stronger evidence.

No source below is silently transposed from a Multi opening. External auction
notation has been normalized to the house style: `-` is pass and only opposing
non-pass calls are parenthesized.

## Result in brief

There is no consensus defense. The credible methods separate into six
families:

| Family | Immediate `X` | Constructive machinery | Best exact source | Principal unresolved choice |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Neutral pass + negative/Stayman-like double | invite+ values; pass keeps later takeout | transfers for every suit; natural `2M`; explicit minor two-suiters | Kokish–Kraft | opener's full pass-or-correct duties |
| Major-showing penalty process | 4+ cards in at least one major, invite+; later doubles penalty | natural `2M`, Rubensohl above | Gilles–Roupoil | exact opener duties after the pass-or-correct call |
| Transfer compression | transfer to hearts | transfers through `3♣`; `2NT` both minors, invite+ | Khandelwal–Khandelwal | everything from `3♦` upward and all delayed auctions |
| Cards/negative double | values without a natural call | natural two-level calls plus Lebensohl/transfer Lebensohl | Larry Cohen; Robinson panel | how to ask for the still-unknown major stopper |
| Replacement Stayman | Stayman | otherwise natural/Lebensohl | Pattaya | source's `2♦` can instead be a minor; no delayed table |
| Called-suit double | diamond length/strength, penalty/lead-directional | full Rubensohl transfers | Glasnevin | diamond-oriented calls do not address the actual unknown major |

That diversity is not merely nomenclature. After `1NT (2♦) - (2♥) - - X`,
published methods make the delayed double either takeout or penalty. After
`1NT (2♦) X (2♥)`, other methods make a later double penalty because the first
double already showed cards or a major. A complete implementation must specify
both auctions separately.

## Exact-object methods

### 1. Kokish–Kraft: neutral pass, negative double, and four-suit transfers

The Eric Kokish–Beverly Kraft system notes contain a separate table for
`1NT (2♦ = 6+♠ or 6+♥)`, expressly classified as one unknown, unbid suit with a
major certain. This is the exact BBA object, but the document is a weak-notrump
system, so its strength thresholds are not automatically portable to pons.
The copy found is an unaffiliated mirror whose filename supplies the January
2008 date
([Kokish–Kraft notes, printed p. 163](https://bridgewithdan.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/WEAK-NOTRUMP-SYSTEM-Kokish-Kraft-Jan-2008.pdf)).

| `1NT (2♦) ?` | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `-` | Neutral; a later `X` is takeout, later `2NT` natural/competitive, and later `3m` competitive ([Kokish–Kraft, p. 163](https://bridgewithdan.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/WEAK-NOTRUMP-SYSTEM-Kokish-Kraft-Jan-2008.pdf)). |
| `X` | Negative and Stayman-like, invitational or better; a later `X` is cooperative penalty and a later `2NT` natural invitational ([Kokish–Kraft, p. 163](https://bridgewithdan.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/WEAK-NOTRUMP-SYSTEM-Kokish-Kraft-Jan-2008.pdf)). |
| `2♥`, `2♠` | Natural and competitive ([Kokish–Kraft, p. 163](https://bridgewithdan.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/WEAK-NOTRUMP-SYSTEM-Kokish-Kraft-Jan-2008.pdf)). |
| `2NT` | Transfer to clubs, invitational or better ([Kokish–Kraft, p. 163](https://bridgewithdan.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/WEAK-NOTRUMP-SYSTEM-Kokish-Kraft-Jan-2008.pdf)). |
| `3♣` | Transfer to diamonds, invitational or better ([Kokish–Kraft, p. 163](https://bridgewithdan.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/WEAK-NOTRUMP-SYSTEM-Kokish-Kraft-Jan-2008.pdf)). |
| `3♦`, `3♥` | Transfers to hearts and spades respectively, invitational or better ([Kokish–Kraft, p. 163](https://bridgewithdan.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/WEAK-NOTRUMP-SYSTEM-Kokish-Kraft-Jan-2008.pdf)). |
| `3♠` | Both minors, game forcing ([Kokish–Kraft, p. 163](https://bridgewithdan.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/WEAK-NOTRUMP-SYSTEM-Kokish-Kraft-Jan-2008.pdf)). |
| `3NT` | Natural, no stopper information, often a gamble based on a long suit ([Kokish–Kraft, p. 163](https://bridgewithdan.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/WEAK-NOTRUMP-SYSTEM-Kokish-Kraft-Jan-2008.pdf)). |
| `4♣`; `4♦`/`4♥`; `4♠` | Gerber; Texas; both minors weak and very long, respectively ([Kokish–Kraft, p. 163](https://bridgewithdan.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/WEAK-NOTRUMP-SYSTEM-Kokish-Kraft-Jan-2008.pdf)). |

The minor-transfer follow-ups distinguish single-suit and two-suit hands. After
the club transfer, `3♦` shows clubs+hearts, `3♥` clubs+spades, `3♠` clubs+diamonds,
and `3NT` a six-card club suit; after the diamond transfer, `3♥` shows
diamonds+spades, `3♠` diamonds+hearts, and `3NT` a six-card diamond suit
([Kokish–Kraft, p. 163](https://bridgewithdan.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/WEAK-NOTRUMP-SYSTEM-Kokish-Kraft-Jan-2008.pdf)).

This table cleanly separates direct and delayed double: `1NT (2♦) X (2M) - - X`
is cooperative penalty, while `1NT (2♦) - (2M) - - X` is takeout. It still does
not give a complete opener-action table at `1NT (2♦) X (2M) ?`
([Kokish–Kraft, p. 163](https://bridgewithdan.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/WEAK-NOTRUMP-SYSTEM-Kokish-Kraft-Jan-2008.pdf)).

### 2. Gilles–Roupoil: show a major, then switch doubles to penalty

The 2006 Gilles–Roupoil system is unusually explicit and labels the auction
“intervention by a Multi-Landy `2♦`.” Their `1NT` opening is weak—11–14 HCP in
first through third seat not vulnerable and 12–14 otherwise—so its strength
threshold is not automatically portable to pons's strong notrump
([Gilles–Roupoil, §§2.1 and 2.3.3](https://www.normalesup.org/~glafon/bridge/gilles.pdf)).
The same notes define their Multi-Landy `2♦` as one six-card major, with
`2M`/`3M`/`4M` pass-or-correct responses, confirming that this is the exact
object rather than an any-one-suiter variant
([Gilles–Roupoil, §13, p. 24](https://www.normalesup.org/~glafon/bridge/gilles.pdf)).

| `1NT (2♦) ?` | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `X` | At least one four-card major and at least invitational values; subsequent doubles are penalty ([Gilles–Roupoil §2.3.3](https://www.normalesup.org/~glafon/bridge/gilles.pdf)). |
| `2♥`, `2♠` | Natural, to play ([Gilles–Roupoil §2.3.3](https://www.normalesup.org/~glafon/bridge/gilles.pdf)). |
| `2NT` and higher | Rubensohl ([Gilles–Roupoil §2.3.3](https://www.normalesup.org/~glafon/bridge/gilles.pdf)). |
| `-`, then a suit | Natural and exactly invitational ([Gilles–Roupoil §2.3.3](https://www.normalesup.org/~glafon/bridge/gilles.pdf)). |
| `-`, then `X` after the major is named | Takeout. The source adds `sans 4AM`; this probably means “without four cards in the other major,” but the abbreviation is not expanded there, so that shape gloss remains **translation uncertainty**, not a settled agreement ([Gilles–Roupoil §2.3.3](https://www.normalesup.org/~glafon/bridge/gilles.pdf)). |

Thus `1NT (2♦) X (2♥) - - X` is penalty in this method, while
`1NT (2♦) - (2♥) - - X` is takeout. The source does not publish a complete
opener rebid table after either double
([Gilles–Roupoil §2.3.3](https://www.normalesup.org/~glafon/bridge/gilles.pdf)).

### 3. *Système Jean Christophe*: a fuller penalty-process structure

The public *Système Jean Christophe* book gives a rich exact-object table. The
same book defines its Multi-Landy `2♦` overcall as a six-card major, confirming
the scope; its counter-defense treats the initial double as showing a major and
beginning a penalty process, yet retains natural weak competition and transfer
machinery
([*Système Jean Christophe*, pp. 61 and 81](https://www.clairebridge.com/textes/jean_christophe.pdf)).

| `1NT (2♦) ?` | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `X` | At least one four-card major, 8+ HCP, any distribution; starts the penalty process ([*Système Jean Christophe*, p. 81](https://www.clairebridge.com/textes/jean_christophe.pdf)). |
| `2♥`, `2♠` | Natural, non-forcing ([*Système Jean Christophe*, p. 81](https://www.clairebridge.com/textes/jean_christophe.pdf)). |
| `2NT` | Transfer to clubs, strength unspecified; in the game-going range it denies a four-card major ([*Système Jean Christophe*, p. 81](https://www.clairebridge.com/textes/jean_christophe.pdf)). |
| `3♣` | Transfer to diamonds, with the same strength and major-suit qualification as `2NT` ([*Système Jean Christophe*, p. 81](https://www.clairebridge.com/textes/jean_christophe.pdf)). |
| `3♦`, `3♥` | Game-forcing transfers. The subsection does not name the target suits, so assigning normal next-step targets is an **inference**, not a quoted agreement ([*Système Jean Christophe*, p. 81](https://www.clairebridge.com/textes/jean_christophe.pdf)). |
| `3NT` | 9+–15 HCP and does not promise a stopper ([*Système Jean Christophe*, p. 81](https://www.clairebridge.com/textes/jean_christophe.pdf)). |
| `-`, then `X` | Takeout, 8+ HCP, without a four-card major ([*Système Jean Christophe*, p. 81](https://www.clairebridge.com/textes/jean_christophe.pdf)). |

After the club or diamond transfer, a further `3♥` shows a spade singleton,
`3♠` a heart singleton, and `3NT` a singleton in the other minor
([*Système Jean Christophe*, p. 81](https://www.clairebridge.com/textes/jean_christophe.pdf)).

The source also states what may follow the initial double: a natural call with
a five-card major as inferred from the auction, another double for penalty, a
cue-bid asking for the stopper needed for `3NT`, or `3NT` showing that stopper
([*Système Jean Christophe*, p. 81](https://www.clairebridge.com/textes/jean_christophe.pdf)).
That is a useful continuation grammar, but it is not a seat-by-seat table for
every `2♥`/`2♠` pass-or-correct branch. In particular, the source does not list
direct four-level actions in this subsection.

### 4. Khandelwal–Khandelwal: preserve transfers by compressing them down one call

Himani and Rajeev Khandelwal's published supplementary partnership notes label
the exact object `1NT (2♦ = mono-M)` and give this compact structure
([Khandelwal–Khandelwal notes, p. 10](https://www.bridgewebs.com/bfame/INDIA%20MIX%201%20HIMANIRAJEEV%202.pdf)):

| `1NT (2♦) ?` | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `X` | Transfer to hearts ([Khandelwal–Khandelwal, p. 10](https://www.bridgewebs.com/bfame/INDIA%20MIX%201%20HIMANIRAJEEV%202.pdf)). |
| `2♥` | Transfer to spades ([Khandelwal–Khandelwal, p. 10](https://www.bridgewebs.com/bfame/INDIA%20MIX%201%20HIMANIRAJEEV%202.pdf)). |
| `2♠` | Transfer to clubs ([Khandelwal–Khandelwal, p. 10](https://www.bridgewebs.com/bfame/INDIA%20MIX%201%20HIMANIRAJEEV%202.pdf)). |
| `2NT` | Both minors, invitational or stronger ([Khandelwal–Khandelwal, p. 10](https://www.bridgewebs.com/bfame/INDIA%20MIX%201%20HIMANIRAJEEV%202.pdf)). |
| `3♣` | Transfer to diamonds ([Khandelwal–Khandelwal, p. 10](https://www.bridgewebs.com/bfame/INDIA%20MIX%201%20HIMANIRAJEEV%202.pdf)). |

The notes introduce the surrounding section as Rubensohl, but this five-call
block supplies no meanings for `3♦`, `3♥`, `3♠`, `3NT`, cues, jumps, or any
continuation after the opponents name their major. Those calls are **gaps**;
they should not be filled from the adjacent defenses to different overcall
meanings without a partnership source
([Khandelwal–Khandelwal, p. 10](https://www.bridgewebs.com/bfame/INDIA%20MIX%201%20HIMANIRAJEEV%202.pdf)).

### 5. Washington Bridge League: simple, complex, and transfer variants

Clyde Kruskal's Washington Bridge League article is explicitly about
`1NT (2♦)` showing one major. It presents two expert-suggested defenses. The
simple defense is only `X = Stayman` and `2NT = Lebensohl`
([Kruskal, WBL Bulletin, Nov./Dec. 2010, pp. 16 and 18](https://www.bridgewebs.com/unit147washingtonleague/November-December%202010.pdf)).

The fuller defense is:

| `1NT (2♦) ?` | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `X` | A good hand, invitational or better, forcing the partnership to `2NT` or a doubled contract; opener may bid game with a maximum ([Kruskal, pp. 18–19](https://www.bridgewebs.com/unit147washingtonleague/November-December%202010.pdf)). |
| `2♥`, `2♠` | Natural, to play, five or more cards ([Kruskal, p. 18](https://www.bridgewebs.com/unit147washingtonleague/November-December%202010.pdf)). |
| `2NT` | Forces `3♣`, permitting a signoff in clubs or diamonds; later `3♥`/`3♠` are club/diamond slam tries ([Kruskal, p. 18](https://www.bridgewebs.com/unit147washingtonleague/November-December%202010.pdf)). |
| `3♣` | Stayman, as after a `2NT` opening ([Kruskal, p. 18](https://www.bridgewebs.com/unit147washingtonleague/November-December%202010.pdf)). |
| `3♦`, `3♥` | Transfers to hearts and spades, invitational or better ([Kruskal, p. 18](https://www.bridgewebs.com/unit147washingtonleague/November-December%202010.pdf)). |
| `3♠` | The partnership's normal meaning after `2NT - 3♠` ([Kruskal, p. 18](https://www.bridgewebs.com/unit147washingtonleague/November-December%202010.pdf)). |
| `3NT` | Natural; neither promises nor denies a stopper ([Kruskal, p. 18](https://www.bridgewebs.com/unit147washingtonleague/November-December%202010.pdf)). |
| `-`, then `X` | Takeout ([Kruskal, p. 18](https://www.bridgewebs.com/unit147washingtonleague/November-December%202010.pdf)). |
| `-`, then `2NT` | Lebensohl, probably en route to `3NT` with a stopper ([Kruskal, p. 18](https://www.bridgewebs.com/unit147washingtonleague/November-December%202010.pdf)). |

After the initial values double, a double by either hand is penalty, while
`2NT` by the doubler is invitational
([Kruskal, p. 18](https://www.bridgewebs.com/unit147washingtonleague/November-December%202010.pdf)).
Kruskal then gives his own variation: use `3♣`/`3♦`/`3♥`/`3♠` as transfers to
diamonds/hearts/spades/clubs, all invitational or better; this frees the slow
`2NT` route followed by `3M` to show a stopper. He also prefers better-minor
Lebensohl, in which `2NT` asks opener to choose a minor
([Kruskal, p. 19](https://www.bridgewebs.com/unit147washingtonleague/November-December%202010.pdf)).

Barry Bragin's adjacent article supplies another exact variant: `X` transfers
to hearts, `2♥` transfers to spades, `2♠` asks opener's strength and may contain
a balanced invite or a club hand, and `2NT` asks for opener's better minor and
may contain both minors or a diamond hand. Direct `3m` is invitational, `3M`
is a minor two-suiter with shortness in the bid major, and `3NT` has game values
without interest in defending
([Bragin, WBL Bulletin, p. 19](https://www.bridgewebs.com/unit147washingtonleague/November-December%202010.pdf)).
Bragin recommends that balanced hands pass first and then use ordinary
Lebensohl after the opponents reveal `2M`; that is a complete design principle,
but not a full call-by-call delayed table
([Bragin, p. 19](https://www.bridgewebs.com/unit147washingtonleague/November-December%202010.pdf)).

This is an expert-authored unit publication, not a federation card or named
partnership's complete system notes. It is nevertheless exact-object evidence,
unlike generic interference advice.

### 6. Larry Cohen: one low-memory rule for natural and conventional overcalls

Larry Cohen's authored method treats any `2♦`-or-higher interference that does
not specifically show both majors alike. Two-level suit bids are natural and
non-forcing, three-level and notrump calls use Lebensohl or transfer Lebensohl,
and four-level calls retain the partnership's front-of-card methods
([Cohen, “Notrump Interference – Advanced”](https://www.larryco.com/bridge-articles/notrump-interference-advanced)).

For this one-unknown-major `2♦`, immediate `X` is cards/negative: values to act,
without promising a particular other major. Pass followed by double after the
opponents disclose a suit is light takeout/competitive; a true penalty hand
passes and hopes opener can reopen with a double
([Cohen, “Notrump Interference – Advanced”](https://www.larryco.com/bridge-articles/notrump-interference-advanced)).

This is a coherent exact-object skeleton—`X` cards, `2M` natural non-forcing,
`2NT` Lebensohl, delayed `X` takeout—but the article does not give a special
unknown-major stopper ask. Applying an ordinary cue/stopper rule to artificial
diamonds would therefore require an additional agreement, not merely the cited
method.

## Near-object methods: useful, but do not transpose silently

### 7. Pattaya: replacement Stayman

Pattaya's one-page defense assigns `X` as Stayman, `3♦` as natural and forcing,
pass as generally below invitational strength, and all other actions as
basically natural with Lebensohl
([Pattaya defense sheet](https://www.pattayabridge.com/conventions/Defense_MultiLandy.pdf)).

This is the cleanest published “stolen `2♦`” treatment found, but Pattaya's
underlying `2♦` permits any six-card suit, not only a major. It says a major is
usual and explains that an occasional minor may force the overcalling side to
the three-level
([Pattaya's Multi-Landy definition](https://www.pattayabridge.com/conventions/MultiLandy_main.htm)).
The defense sheet delegates Lebensohl continuations to another document and
does not specify what doubles mean after a pass-or-correct `2M`
([Pattaya defense sheet](https://www.pattayabridge.com/conventions/Defense_MultiLandy.pdf)).

### 8. Glasnevin: Rubensohl, but double the artificial suit bid

Glasnevin Bridge Club's Rubensohl note keeps available two-level calls natural
and to play. `2NT` transfers to clubs, `3♣` to diamonds, `3♦` to hearts, and
`3♥` to spades; the transfer into the overcaller's suit becomes a Stayman-like
transfer cue. Direct `3♠` is invitational-or-better and denies a stopper or a
four-card major, while direct `3NT` is game-going, denies a four-card major, and
shows a stopper
([Glasnevin Rubensohl note](https://www.glasnevinbridgeclub.com/styled-3/styled-4/styled-37/index.html)).

For a conventional call showing unspecified suits—its example is Multi-Landy
`2♦`—Glasnevin makes `X` show length and strength in the **called suit**, hence
penalty/lead-directional diamonds rather than cards or a major
([Glasnevin Rubensohl note](https://www.glasnevinbridgeclub.com/styled-3/styled-4/styled-37/index.html)).

This is internally explicit for double but externally mismatched to BBA's
actual threat: diamonds is not one of the possible strains. The note does not
say whether its generic “stopper” language over artificial `2♦` refers to
diamonds, to the eventual major, or to some later disclosure. That mapping is a
**source gap**.

## Official-card evidence and its limit

The 2026 Eurobridge card for Djordjević–Gorana defines their own Multi-Landy
`2♦` as a six-card-or-longer major. Its separate generic `1NT (2x)` Lebensohl
note says `X` is negative, two-level `2y` is non-forcing, and `2NT` forces
`3♣`; slow and direct cue/`3NT` routes distinguish hands with and without a
stopper
([official Eurobridge convention card, pp. 1 and 6](https://championships.eurobridge.org/2026EOWT/CC/Serbia1_Djordjevic_Gorana.pdf)).

The card never states that this generic table is overridden—or applied
literally—when the interfering `2♦` is an unknown-major Multi. In particular,
“stopper in x” could mean the called diamonds under a literal reading, while
the bridge problem is stopping hearts and/or spades. It is therefore strong
evidence for **negative double + Lebensohl as an elite partnership's generic
base**, but not a complete `2♦`-specific counter-defense
([official Eurobridge convention card, p. 6](https://championships.eurobridge.org/2026EOWT/CC/Serbia1_Djordjevic_Gorana.pdf)).

## Expert evidence: no consensus on double

Steve Robinson's NVBA Bridge Expert Panel asked experts to state their rules
after the partnership's `1NT` is disturbed. The responses support several
incompatible choices rather than one standard:

| Expert | Treatment relevant to `1NT (2♦ Multi)` |
| --- | --- |
| Henry Bethe | Over a purely artificial Multi `2♦` showing one major, direct `X` shows values and cooperative penalty interest; pass then `X` is pure penalty. He explicitly rejects stolen-bid doubles ([Robinson expert panel](https://www.nvba.org/Robinson/articles/defense-against-notrump.html)). |
| Eddie Wold | Over an artificial one- or two-suit call, `X` shows cards and forces through `2NT`; later doubles are penalty ([Robinson expert panel](https://www.nvba.org/Robinson/articles/defense-against-notrump.html)). |
| Marty Bergen | `X` is takeout when the call promises the suit bid, but cards when the call does not promise that suit ([Robinson expert panel](https://www.nvba.org/Robinson/articles/defense-against-notrump.html)). |
| Danny Gerstman | Against Woolsey-like artificial auctions, the first `X` shows cards; if the same hand doubles again, the later double is penalty ([Robinson expert panel](https://www.nvba.org/Robinson/articles/defense-against-notrump.html)). |
| Chip Martel | Negative-type doubles over one-suiters or a shown suit plus unknown companion; card-showing doubles over known two-suiters and artificial calls. A pure artificial one-suiter lies across those verbal categories, illustrating why a specific agreement is still needed ([Robinson expert panel](https://www.nvba.org/Robinson/articles/defense-against-notrump.html)). |
| Nick Nickell | `X` of `2♦`, regardless of its meaning, is negative/cards; his published minimum example has 4–4 majors, two diamonds, and three clubs ([Robinson expert panel](https://www.nvba.org/Robinson/articles/defense-against-notrump.html)). |
| John Mohan | Initial doubles are card-showing and mildly invitational; after a card-showing or balancing double, all later doubles are penalty. He says major-showing artificial calls need a specific transfer-Lebensohl defense ([Robinson expert panel](https://www.nvba.org/Robinson/articles/defense-against-notrump.html)). |

Fred Hamilton supplies a simple transfer-Lebensohl base in the same panel:
two-level calls natural and non-forcing, `2NT` forcing `3♣`, then `3♣` Stayman,
red-suit calls transfers, `3♠` whatever the partnership normally plays after
`2NT - 3♠`, and `3NT` to play
([Robinson expert panel](https://www.nvba.org/Robinson/articles/defense-against-notrump.html)).
That base is not presented as a complete unknown-major tail.

The panel's practical conclusion is evidence, not inference: reputable experts
play both penalty-process and negative/cards doubles, and they disagree even
about whether a delayed double is takeout or pure penalty
([Robinson expert panel](https://www.nvba.org/Robinson/articles/defense-against-notrump.html)).

## Secondary taxonomy

A BBO expert-forum discussion is useful only as a map of the design space. It
lists (1) stolen-bid `X = Stayman`, with natural/Lebensohl or Rubensohl calls;
(2) a Dixon-like Multi defense with `X = values`, weak `2M` takeouts, and
naturals elsewhere; and (3) a defensive double that establishes penalty
doubles. Other named contributors describe a strong balanced direct double
with delayed takeout, or transfer-Lebensohl with natural `2M`
([BBO discussion](https://www.bridgebase.com/forums/topic/47404-countering-multi-landy-2d-over-a-weak-nt/)).
These posts corroborate the families above but are not used to fill any missing
continuation in a primary method.

## The pass-or-correct decision tree

The most important continuation is not the first call but the relationship
between the first and second doubles:

| Agreement family | `1NT (2♦) X (2♥) - - X` | `1NT (2♦) - (2♥) - - X` | What is still missing? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kokish–Kraft | Cooperative penalty after the direct negative/Stayman-like `X` ([p. 163](https://bridgewithdan.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/WEAK-NOTRUMP-SYSTEM-Kokish-Kraft-Jan-2008.pdf)). | Takeout after a neutral direct pass ([p. 163](https://bridgewithdan.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/WEAK-NOTRUMP-SYSTEM-Kokish-Kraft-Jan-2008.pdf)). | Opener's exact choices over the first pass-or-correct call. |

There is a second distinction after `1NT (2♦) X (2♥) ?`: should opener double
with four hearts, pass to let responder decide, show a four-card major, or make
a natural notrump call? None of the exact-object external sources above gives a
complete opener table for that position. That is a **research gap**, not license
to splice one source's first round onto another source's tail.

This is a bounded negative finding about this source set, not a claim that no
partnership anywhere plays pons's calls.

## Reconciliation with BBA and pons

### What BBA actually does

The empirical BBA probe is more complete than any public prose source for the
specific anchor. BBA's `2♦` is one six-card major with sampled 9–18 HCP and a
13-HCP median—not merely a textbook weak two
([BBA Multi distillation](bba-multi-2d.md)). Its advancer never passes the first
double: it chooses `2♥` as the weak pass-or-correct route or a stronger,
spade-tolerant `2♠` route
([BBA Multi distillation](bba-multi-2d.md)).

At `1NT (2♦) ?`, BBA uses `X` about 41% as values/takeout, `2NT` as a largely
balanced natural-invite-like call, natural weak `2M`, natural constructive
three-level suits, and natural game jumps
([BBA Multi distillation](bba-multi-2d.md); [counter-defense census](bba-1nt-counter-defense.md)).
After `1NT (2♦) X (2M)`, opener overwhelmingly passes and never doubles the
named major in the probe. Responder can later double the resolved major for
takeout; if overcaller instead runs from `(2♥)` to `(2♠)`, that later spade
double is penalty
([BBA Multi distillation](bba-multi-2d.md)).

That BBA combination does **not** match a single published family above. Its
first `X` resembles Cohen's cards/negative double and the panel's values
double, while its natural `2NT` conflicts with every Lebensohl/Rubensohl relay
and with Khandelwal's both-minors call. The *function* of its later takeout
double resembles the pass-then-double treatment in Gilles, Jean Christophe,
and Cohen, but the timing does not: BBA has already made the direct values `X`,
whereas those three takeout doubles follow a direct pass. Its special penalty
double after a run to spades is another empirical BBA tail
([BBA Multi distillation](bba-multi-2d.md)).

### What pons currently does

Pons now ships an adapted Kokish–Kraft table default-on against a declared
Multi. It preserves the source's defining split: after the initial values `X`,
a repeated double is cooperative penalty; after an initial neutral pass, the
delayed double is takeout. Its exact thresholds, transfer adaptations,
interfered tails, A/B verdict, and remaining differences from the source live
in the [current `1NT (2♦)` tree](../one-notrump-multi.md) and
[campaign §N4-KK](../one-notrump-competitive.md#n4-kk--the-kokishkraft-counter-a-whole-table-variant-shipped-default-on-2026-08-25).

The retired v7 fallback (`--no-ns-multi-kokish-kraft`) is the BBA-like hybrid
described above: values `X` at 6+ HCP, a delayed takeout double after the major
is resolved, and a penalty double after a heart-to-spade run.

## Candidate packages that the evidence actually supports

This was the pre-implementation candidate list. Candidate 1 shipped in adapted
form on 2026-08-25; the others remain research alternatives, not
recommendations to ship:

1. **Kokish–Kraft four-suit-transfer package (shipped in adapted form):**
   neutral pass with delayed takeout, invite+ negative/Stayman-like `X` with
   later cooperative penalty doubles, natural `2M`, four-suit transfers, `3♠`
   both minors GF, and explicit game-level calls. This is the most complete
   exact-object package found
   ([Kokish–Kraft](https://bridgewithdan.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/WEAK-NOTRUMP-SYSTEM-Kokish-Kraft-Jan-2008.pdf)).
2. **Gilles/Jean-Christophe penalty process:** `X` guarantees a four-card major
   and invite-or-better values; `2M` natural; transfer/Rubensohl calls above;
   all doubles after direct `X` penalty, but pass-then-double takeout
   ([Gilles–Roupoil](https://www.normalesup.org/~glafon/bridge/gilles.pdf);
   [*Jean Christophe*](https://www.clairebridge.com/textes/jean_christophe.pdf)).
3. **Khandelwal transfer compression:** `X`→hearts, `2♥`→spades,
   `2♠`→clubs, `2NT` both minors invite+, `3♣`→diamonds. This candidate is
   incomplete until the unresolved calls and competitive tails are authored
   independently
   ([Khandelwal–Khandelwal](https://www.bridgewebs.com/bfame/INDIA%20MIX%201%20HIMANIRAJEEV%202.pdf)).
4. **Kruskal simple or complex defense:** at minimum `X = Stayman` plus
   Lebensohl; in the fuller form, `X` is invite+ values and forces through
   `2NT` or a doubled contract, direct `2M` are natural, direct three-level
   calls preserve the `2NT` structure, and pass-then-double is takeout
   ([Kruskal](https://www.bridgewebs.com/unit147washingtonleague/November-December%202010.pdf)).
5. **Cohen low-memory defense:** `X` cards/negative, natural non-forcing `2M`,
   Lebensohl/transfer Lebensohl, and pass-then-double takeout. It still needs a
   pure-major stopper agreement and force-level details
   ([Cohen](https://www.larryco.com/bridge-articles/notrump-interference-advanced)).
6. **Replacement Stayman:** `X` Stayman, `3♦` natural forcing, other calls
   natural/Lebensohl. This is sourced against a broader any-one-suiter `2♦`, so
   it must be evaluated rather than assumed equivalent
   ([Pattaya defense](https://www.pattayabridge.com/conventions/Defense_MultiLandy.pdf);
   [Pattaya scope](https://www.pattayabridge.com/conventions/MultiLandy_main.htm)).
7. **BBA empirical natural package:** values `X`, natural `2M`/`2NT`/three-level
   calls, plus BBA's measured delayed-double rules. This is reproducible anchor
   evidence rather than a published human convention
   ([BBA Multi distillation](bba-multi-2d.md)).

Before any candidate is implementable, it must answer all of these as one
coherent package:

- Does direct `X` promise a major, general cards, Stayman, a transfer, or
  diamond strength?
- Is `1NT (2♦) X (2M)` forcing on opener, and when may opener convert or double?
- Is `1NT (2♦) - (2M) - - X` takeout or penalty?
- What happens when overcaller corrects `(2♥)` to `(2♠)`?
- Is `2NT` natural, a `3♣` relay, a club transfer, or both minors?
- How are one-major and both-major stoppers shown before and after the major is
  resolved?
- What are direct `3x`, `3NT`, `4♣`, and `4♦`, and what survives interference?

The local measurement rule still governs: literature makes a package
authorable, not shippable. Any bidding change needs complete constructive and
interfered tails and a seeded A/B on both scorers.
